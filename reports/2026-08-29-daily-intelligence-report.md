## Zero Trust Tactics

**[Think You've Eliminated Chinese AI? Check the Model's Lineage, Cisco Says](https://www.securityweek.com/think-youve-eliminated-chinese-ai-check-the-models-lineage-cisco-says/)** (SecurityWeek, reporting on Cisco research) — Cisco published research showing that excluding AI models labeled "Chinese" from a tech stack doesn't guarantee Chinese-origin AI has actually been eliminated, a phenomenon it calls "provenance entanglement." Using its Model Provenance Kit (examining an artifact's internals) alongside VAIL's Behavioral Fingerprinting (examining inference behavior externally), Cisco's researchers found that Nemotron models built from Qwen base weights were substantially more similar to Qwen than chance would predict — post-training and a new publisher name don't reliably erase a detectable relationship to an upstream model family. Cisco frames the risk in software-supply-chain terms: if an upstream model is later found to carry a backdoor, systematic bias, or exploitable behavior, organizations need to know which downstream derivatives inherited it, the same problem an SBOM solves for traditional code dependencies.

**[Claude, Codex, and Hermes Installed Unowned Code Inside Corporate Networks](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/claude-codex-and-hermes-installed-unowned-code-inside-corporate-networks/)** (Ars Technica) — Researchers at an Israeli stealth startup scanned 6,214 live domains belonging to defense contractors, Fortune 500s, and Big Tech companies, finding 8,265 `llms.txt`/`llms-full.txt` files (an emerging machine-readable-summary convention, the AI equivalent of `robots.txt`). Of these, 120 files across as many sites pointed to unregistered code packages or domain names. The researchers registered a handful of the unclaimed names; within an hour they received a phone-home response from a Fortune 500 company, and more followed from other Fortune 500s and startups — the beacon's process-chain data confirmed coding agents, including Claude, OpenAI's Codex, and Nous Research's Hermes, had installed the packages autonomously. At least one active attack is already exploiting the same confusion: an `llms.txt` file on the legitimate site clerk.com referenced an `npx` command whose package name had since been claimed by an attacker and used to host live malware (Clerk has since fixed it). "The trust model is broken," one researcher said — coding agents treat vendor documentation as authoritative and don't verify that a referenced package or domain actually exists or belongs to the company, and endpoint detection sees nothing unusual because the install looks like an authorized package manager run by the company's own coding agent. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Nous Research had not responded to requests for comment as of publication.

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**[Judge Blocks Pentagon Blacklist of Anthropic AI](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/28/judge-blocks-pentagon-anthropic-blacklist)** (Axios) — US District Judge Rita Lin ruled that the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply-chain risk was unlawful, finding in a 59-page order that the action "constituted unlawful retaliation" against Anthropic in violation of its First Amendment rights and denied the company due process under the Fifth Amendment. The dispute stemmed from Anthropic's refusal to permit Pentagon use of its tools for mass surveillance or lethal autonomous weapons; Judge Lin wrote that "the empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics." Anthropic was the first company publicly designated a supply-chain risk under the statute the Pentagon invoked. The ruling is a win in the San Francisco case specifically — a separate, related piece of litigation in Washington, D.C. is still ongoing, and Anthropic technically remains designated a supply-chain risk until that case resolves.

## Risk Forecasting Tactics

**[Tech, Cybersecurity Giants Unite Behind OpenAI-Led Cyber Defense Pledge](https://www.securityweek.com/tech-cybersecurity-giants-unite-behind-openai-led-cyber-defense-pledge/)** (SecurityWeek; also reported by [Wired's weekly security roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-the-cybersecurity-apocalypse-is-coming-in-months-ai-giants-warn/), sourcing the letter to Axios, and corroborated by [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 165](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/165)) — Nearly 130 technology and cybersecurity companies, led by OpenAI and including Anthropic, Microsoft, Google, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, IBM, Capital One, Mastercard, and Visa, signed an open letter calling for a coordinated global surge in cyber defense, warning that AI-enabled attacks will become significantly more capable "in the coming months." The letter calls on organizations to make cyber defense an "immediate leadership priority" and on governments to give hospitals, water utilities, and local governments access to capable defensive AI, as well as to "impose costs" on attackers. Wired's own coverage notes the letter includes no specific commitments, deadlines, or funding pledges. The warning follows a summer marked by the OpenAI/Hugging Face AI-agent incident and a coordinated cyberattack on Michigan water infrastructure in early August.
