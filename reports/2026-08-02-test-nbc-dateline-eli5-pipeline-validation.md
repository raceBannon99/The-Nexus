## Bradlee — So, what's the answer?

In 2007, at DEF CON 15, an NBC Dateline producer named Michelle Madigan tried to secretly film attendees with a hidden camera, hoping to catch people admitting to illegal hacking on tape — reportedly for a segment about "hackers for hire." DEF CON's organizers offered her a normal press badge, which would have let her film and interview openly, on four separate occasions. She declined every time and instead registered as a regular attendee with a camera hidden in a small bag.

It didn't work. DEF CON had advance warning she was coming, and a staffer caught her covertly filming attendees during the conference's Capture the Flag hacking competition. Later that day, during a session, founder Jeff Moss took the stage, told the room a hidden-camera reporter was in the crowd, and turned it into a live game: "Spot the Undercover Press." Before the room could even respond, Madigan got up and fled — and roughly 150 people, including actual journalists covering the conference, chased her out of the hotel and through the parking lot to her car.

The story she'd come to find — hackers admitting to crimes on hidden camera — never materialized. Instead, she became the story, and it's remembered today as one of DEF CON's most famous examples of the community policing its own openness.

Why it landed the way it did: DEF CON had already offered the honest path repeatedly and been refused, which is what turned "reporter gets caught" into "reporter gets embarrassed" rather than "conference is hostile to press." And the on-stage "outing" wasn't improvised out of nothing — it's the same mechanism as DEF CON's older "Spot the Fed" game, aimed at a covert journalist instead of a plainclothes federal agent: someone at the conference under false pretenses, publicly called out in front of the whole room, as both entertainment and deterrent.

What's still genuinely fuzzy, and doesn't change the substance of the story: exactly which session Moss made the announcement during (sources disagree slightly), and whether NBC's segment ever actually aired — no source, in this pass or the original, answers that.
