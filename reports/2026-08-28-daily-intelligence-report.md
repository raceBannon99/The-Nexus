## Adversary Playbook

**[CISA Attributes 100+ Water System Attacks to Iranian Threat Actors, Updating a Five-Week-Old Vague Tracking Entry](https://www.securityweek.com/cisa-over-100-internet-exposed-water-systems-targeted-in-july-cyberattacks/)** (SecurityWeek; corroborated by [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 164](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/164)) — CISA disclosed that Iranian threat actors targeted more than 100 internet-exposed water and wastewater systems in July 2026, exploiting programmable logic controllers connected directly to cellular modems to gain unauthorized OT access. At least 12 states were affected, with Minnesota, Michigan, South Dakota, Georgia, New Jersey, and Alabama confirmed; the attacks did not cause significant disruption. CISA recommends identifying internet-accessible systems, eliminating unnecessary exposure, and — for critical systems — default password changes, patching, secure remote-access gateways, MFA, and continuous traffic monitoring. This updates this Nexus's own Adversary Tracking Report: the Iran table's OT/ICS-targeting row has carried only "Rockwell/Allen-Bradley, Schneider Electric, Siemens PLCs" with no attribution or scale since 07-23 — today's disclosure supplies both. *Also covered below under Critical Infrastructure Attacks.*

**[APT28's HOOKEDGE Backdoor Targets European Government and Diplomatic Organizations](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/apt28-linked-hookedge-backdoor-targets.html)** (The Hacker News) — Researchers disclosed HOOKEDGE, a lightweight Windows batch-script backdoor attributed with moderate confidence to APT28 (Fancy Bear/GRU, tracked by Recorded Future as BlueDelta) on the strength of code and tradecraft overlap with the group's prior HEADLACE backdoor. The campaign, active from September 2025 through April 2026, targeted government and diplomatic organizations in Romania, Spain, and Türkiye via macro-enabled Word documents with diplomatic-themed lures; later variants used webhook[.]site services for C2 and beaconed as often as every five minutes via headless Microsoft Edge instances. New row added to this Nexus's Adversary Tracking Report (Russia table).

**[Foreign Intelligence Services Now Blamed for Nearly 4 in 10 Cyberattacks on German Companies, Up From 7% in 2023](https://therecord.media/germany-cyberattacks-china-russia)** (The Record, citing a Bitkom survey of 1,003 German companies) — Germany's digital-industry association found that 38% of German companies hit by data theft, industrial espionage, or sabotage over the past year attributed at least one incident to a foreign intelligence service — up from 28% last year and 7% in 2023, now the second-most-blamed attacker category behind organized crime. Of affected companies, 52% traced at least one attack to China, 49% to Russia, and 9% to Iran. Sinan Selen, president of Germany's domestic intelligence agency, said foreign services "have intensified their hybrid activities and are increasingly responsible for attacks on the German economy." An aggregate trend rather than a single named campaign, so no new Adversary Tracking Report row — reported here in the body instead.

**["Server Killers" Claims Responsibility for the Norwegian DDoS Attack This Nexus Covered Unattributed Two Days Ago](https://www.securityweek.com/pro-russian-hackers-claim-responsibility-for-major-cyberattack-on-norways-public-digital-services/)** (SecurityWeek) — A self-branded pro-Russian group calling itself "Server Killers" claimed via Telegram to have carried out the three-day denial-of-service campaign against Norway's Digitalisation Agency (Digdir) covered in the 08-26 report, framing it as a declaration of "cyber war" after Norway renewed security cooperation with Ukraine on August 23rd. Norwegian officials had not confirmed the claim by publication time. The attack was, per Digdir, "the biggest attack against Digdir solutions" the agency has experienced, two to three times the scale of two earlier 2026 incidents against the same infrastructure. New row added to this Nexus's Adversary Tracking Report (Hacktivism table, Tier 3 given the unconfirmed self-claim) — filed as an attribution update to the already-tracked incident, not a new one.

**[Qilin Ransomware Gang Claims ATF Breach; DOJ Confirms "Major Incident," Doesn't Confirm Attribution](https://therecord.media/doj-atf-cyberattack-qilin-ransomware)** (The Record) — The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives confirmed a cyberattack on a standalone computer system holding information about investigation targets — isolated from case-management, laboratory, and eForms systems, and shut down when discovered — as the Qilin ransomware gang claimed the breach on its leak site. Senior DOJ officials designated the incident "major" under federal guidelines, triggering department-level involvement; the agency says the investigation is ongoing and has released no further detail. New row added to this Nexus's Adversary Tracking Report (Cybercrime table, Tier 3 — Qilin's own leak-site claim is not yet independently confirmed).

**[A New York Media Firm Built a Fake Think Tank to Feed 560,000 Words of Pro-Israel Content to AI Chatbots](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2655943/media)** (Arab News, corroborating The Guardian's original reporting — Guardian's own site blocks direct fetch/search; cited via N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 164, and independent DOJ FARA-filing records) — Piro Inc., a New York media production company registered under the Foreign Agents Registration Act as an Israeli-government foreign agent (having received roughly $900,000 for the work), built the "Hanover Institute for Public Policy" — a think tank with no legal entity, physical address, named staff, or report bylines — which published 124 reports totaling more than 560,000 words between August 6th and 14th. ## Data Breaches

**[Cyberattack Causes Global Disruption at Boston Scientific](https://www.securityweek.com/cyberattack-causes-global-disruption-at-boston-scientific/)** (SecurityWeek) — Boston Scientific, a medical-device manufacturer making cardiology, neurology, oncology, and interventional equipment, disclosed a cyberattack detected August 25th that disrupted access to operating systems and business applications, including its ability to process and ship customer orders. The company told the SEC on August 26th that the full scope and impact remain unknown and it cannot yet provide a restoration timeline. No group has claimed responsibility; SecurityWeek reported it had not received a response to a request for ransomware confirmation.

**[Sensitive Information Exposed in Nutex Health Data Breach](https://www.securityweek.com/sensitive-information-exposed-in-nutex-health-data-breach/)** (SecurityWeek) — Nutex Health, a Houston-based healthcare management company operating 28 facilities across 12 states, disclosed in an SEC 8-K filing (August 24th) that an unauthorized third party accessed and exfiltrated files from some of its servers. The company is still determining whether patient, employee, provider, business, financial, or intellectual-property data was among the stolen files, and has reported the incident to law enforcement. As of late August, Nutex says it has found no material impact on operations or financial reporting.

**[Cybercrooks Jet Off With Manchester Airports Group Customer Data — 8.7 Million Records](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/27/cybercrooks-jet-off-with-manchester-airports-group-customer-data/5292943)** (The Register; also reported by N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 164) — Manchester Airports Group discovered on August 25th that an unauthorized party had accessed data belonging to 8.7 million customers of Manchester, Stansted, and East Midlands airports, tied to car-park, lounge, Fast Track, and in-airport WiFi-signup bookings. Exposed data includes email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registration numbers, and postcodes — the majority of affected customers had only an email address exposed, and payment/bank details were not stored on the affected system. MAG notified the UK's National Cyber Security Centre and Information Commissioner's Office; no airport experienced operational disruption, and passenger safety was not affected.

## Zero Trust Tactics

**[Critical cPanel Flaw Could Let One Hosting Customer Take Root Control of a Whole Server](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/critical-cpanel-flaw-could-let-one.html)** (The Hacker News) — CVE-2026-65643 affects cPanel & WHM's domain-parking and addon-domain functionality: an authenticated account holder with permission to add parked or addon domains can create arbitrary files on the server, leading to root-level code execution. Patches shipped August 27th across all supported branches (11.110.0.141+ through 11.138.1.7+); no public exploitation reported and the flaw isn't yet in CISA's KEV catalog, but shared-hosting environments where customers manage their own domains are the primary risk.

**[PaperCut Zero-Day Exploited in Attacks, Affecting All NG and MF Versions](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/papercut-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks.html)** (The Hacker News) — A zero-day in PaperCut's print-management software is under active exploitation across all PaperCut NG and MF versions; PaperCut says it's "aware of confirmed customer incidents." Emergency patches are available for v25 and v26; technical details remain undisclosed, but indicators include suspicious activity from `pc-app.exe`, missing or deleted `server.log` files, and specific JDBC/database error entries. Immediate mitigation is restricting the PaperCut server web interface to trusted IPs.

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**[Trump Order Aims to Block Foreign Backdoors in US Power Grid Gear](https://www.securityweek.com/trump-order-aims-to-block-foreign-backdoors-in-us-power-grid-gear/)** (SecurityWeek; corroborated by [The Record](https://therecord.media/)) — Executive Order 14420 prohibits acquiring, importing, transferring, or installing foreign-produced bulk-power equipment after August 26th where transactions involve entities posing risks of "sabotage, unauthorized access, malicious remote operation, or supply disruption." The order covers transmission infrastructure rated 69kV or higher (excluding local distribution), reaching transformers, inverters, energy-storage systems, and industrial control systems; the Energy Department can require operators to isolate, monitor, secure, disconnect, or replace pre-2026 installations after consulting defense and intelligence officials. No country or company is named, but the structure mirrors a 2020 Trump order that led to a Chinese-entity-targeted DOE prohibition later revoked under the Biden administration.
