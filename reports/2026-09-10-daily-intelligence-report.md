## Adversary Playbook

**Four China-linked espionage groups caught sharing an AI-assisted Chrome exploit kit.** At least four separate cyber-espionage groups — most tied to Chinese state intelligence — used an identical, previously unknown Google Chrome vulnerability in attacks beginning in late August and continuing into this week, cybersecurity firm Proofpoint reported. The exploit kit, dubbed "BlueMoon," chains two Chromium flaws (patched upstream in early August, but not reaching users for four weeks — a "patch gap") with a Windows privilege-escalation bug, then hands off to per-group malware. TA412 (also tracked as APT31, Violet Typhoon, and RedBravo — an established group already hit with a 2024 DOJ indictment and an MSS-front-company sanction) installed a browser extension disguised as Google's Gemini assistant to steal credentials and surveil victims, targeting US NGOs, mining companies, and commodity traders via fake internship and conference lures. A second group, UNK_LateNight, used the kit to install the ShadowPad backdoor against US aerospace and defense companies via fake procurement inquiries. A third, UNK_DoubleCheck, hit a Vietnamese manufacturer via a compromised Southeast Asian government email account, and a fourth, UNK_QuietRacket, targeted government, consulting, and financial organizations in Indonesia and Singapore. Proofpoint's own researchers said the code is "practically identical — even the variable naming, the commentary," ruling out parallel development, and found signs AI tools helped build the exploit from the public patch. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/china-hackers-chrome-browser-zero-day-multiple-groups) — also reported by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/four-spy-groups-used-same-chrome-and.html).

**Google's threat-intelligence arm maps a wave of AI-enabled espionage and crime clusters.** Google's Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) published a report documenting how AI is letting lesser-resourced attackers operate at nation-state scale — including one case where "an AI coding chatbot, a prompt, and a set of agent instructions" planned, built, and executed a mass credential-harvesting campaign in under six hours. The report names several clusters: **UNC6508** (PRC-nexus), running a multi-year cyberespionage campaign against academic, medical, and military research institutions in North America; **Basin Castle** (PRC), using large language models for reconnaissance, social engineering, and malware development; **Ravine Castle**, tracked as **APT24** (PRC), using Google's own Gemini across the full attack lifecycle; and **Midnight Neptune**, tracked as **UNC1069** (North Korea), which has integrated AI tooling into its ongoing cryptocurrency-theft operations. The report also updates two campaigns The Nexus already tracks: **TeamPCP** (the Australian-operated group behind ShadowRay 2.0/IronErn, tracked by GTIG as **UNC6780**) is now linked to Dustmaker, Shai-Hulud-lineage, and Miasma malware distributed via supply-chain compromises of PyPI, npm, and Docker Hub since March 2026 — including the six-hour AI-orchestrated example above; and **APT42** (Iran/IRGC, tracked by GTIG as "Calanque Ion") now deploys generative AI for targeting and localized phishing-lure generation. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-is-giving-lesser-resourced-attackers-nation-state-level-reach-google-warns/).

**US disrupts a $24 billion Chinese-language cybercrime marketplace.** The Treasury Department sanctioned Xinbi Guarantee, a Telegram-based illicit marketplace, and two businesses accused of supporting it, while the Secret Service — with cooperation from Tether and blockchain-intelligence firm Elliptic — seized $52.8 million from 52 cryptocurrency wallets tied to the platform. A federal court also authorized seizure of Xinbi's Telegram channels. Created in 2022, Xinbi processed at least $24 billion in transactions, making it the second-largest illicit online marketplace in history per Elliptic, and hosted more than 4,600 crime-as-a-service vendors offering money laundering, stolen personal data, deepfake technology, and pig-butchering-scam support, with payments in Tether's USDT stablecoin. Xinbi grew out of Telegram's prior takedown of the similar Huione Guarantee marketplace. The DOJ's Scam Center Task Force said it would "expand its scope to attack scam compounds globally," pointing to a related operation that took down 13 scam centers in Madagascar. Xinbi's operators told users on Telegram they would move to a harder-to-seize stablecoin, and Elliptic's cofounder said the platform will likely "try to rebuild and relaunch," though it will be "very challenging in the face of these sanctions." [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/us-disrupts-xinbi-guarantee-marketplace-cybercrime). *Also covered below under Law Enforcement Disruption.*

**Three unrelated actors caught exploiting the same critical Cisco firewall flaw.** SecurityWeek reports that Cisco and CISA have flagged active exploitation of CVE-2026-20079, a critical pre-authentication flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center that allows unauthenticated remote attackers to execute malicious scripts and gain root access — the third FMC vulnerability added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog in 2026 (added September 9, with a September 12 federal patch deadline). Three separate, unrelated actors — tracked individually by Cisco Talos — are exploiting the flaw for different purposes: **UAT-11823**, linked with high confidence to Russia's **Sandworm** (GRU/APT44/Seashell Blizzard), is using it to deliver the Cyclops Blink malware; **UAT-11988**, linked to the **Qilin** ransomware group (already tracked here for its self-claimed ATF breach), is using it for initial access; and an unattributed **UAT-12197** has deployed web shells and malicious JAR files. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/organizations-warned-of-cisco-secure-fmc-exploitation/) — also flagged by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisa-flags-exploited-cisco-citrix.html).

**Wildberries' seller-payment systems disrupted by DDoS, delaying an estimated $240 million.** Russian e-commerce giant Wildberries told local media that a distributed denial-of-service attack against its seller payment- and withdrawal-tracking systems has delayed payouts, leaving merchants owed roughly 20 billion rubles (about $240 million) for late-July sales, according to the Russian Union of Marketplace Sellers. Wildberries did not attribute the DDoS attack or confirm any connection to Ukraine's Main Intelligence Directorate (HUR), which said last month it conducted a combined cyberattack-and-drone-strike operation against the company alongside the hacktivist group Cyber Corps, aiming to amplify the damage from Ukrainian strikes on Wildberries warehouses. The sellers' union pushed back on Wildberries' explanation, saying it was "very convenient to hide behind external factors." [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/russian-e-commerce-giant-wildberries-says-payments-disrupted).

**Ransomware gang "The Gentlemen" claims breach of health-records vendor Veradigm, 3.5 million patients.** Electronic health records company Veradigm told the SEC that hackers used stolen credentials from a vendor partner's environment to access an API and download personal data — including, in some cases, Social Security numbers, though no clinical data — for an undisclosed number of patients. The Gentlemen ransomware gang, a Qilin splinter group already tracked here for its AnMed and Nutex Health attacks, added Veradigm to its leak site on Friday, claiming to have stolen the health records of 3.5 million patients. Veradigm, formerly known as Allscripts, has been hit before: a 2019 SamSam ransomware attack caused hospital outages, and the company reported a separate breach affecting 2.67 million people in December 2024. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/electronic-health-record-company-says-customer-data-stolen-in-breach). *Also covered below under Data Breaches.*

**Hundreds of AI agents used to breach 440+ schools' print-management servers in hours.** A suspected Russian-speaking actor used hundreds of AI agents — built on OpenAI Codex and DeepSeek models, coordinated through a persistent-memory service called "Hindsight" and a unified workspace called "AionUi" — to exploit two PaperCut MF/NG print-management vulnerabilities (an authentication bypass and a remote-code-execution flaw) and compromise at least 440 instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries, primarily schools and universities in the US, UK, France, Spain, Canada, Belgium, Portugal, Australia, Germany, and Switzerland. Researchers say the actor began vulnerability research on August 31 and had a working, multi-threaded exploitation tool within hours; the first real victim was compromised in under four hours, 11 organizations were compromised within 26 seconds of the automated campaign's launch, and one US high school went from initial access to full domain administrator control in seven minutes. The operators also used standard offensive tools including Mimikatz, SharpHound, Certipy, Rubeus, and Impacket. [Read more (The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/papercut-attacker-uses-hundreds-of-ai.html).

**Critical Citrix NetScaler flaw under active exploitation since early September.** SecurityWeek reports active exploitation, since at least September 3, of CVE-2026-19490, a critical (CVSS 9.3) authentication-bypass flaw affecting Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances configured as gateways or AAA virtual servers — following public disclosure of exploit code on GitHub. Citrix patched the flaw on August 19; CISA has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a three-day federal patch mandate under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. No named attacker or campaign has been identified; researchers note NetScaler products are high-value targets that typically see fast post-disclosure exploitation. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/critical-netscaler-vulnerability-exploited-in-attacks/).

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**FBI releases its first-ever public cybersecurity strategy.** The FBI on Wednesday unveiled a 17-page, unclassified cybersecurity strategy — its first public one, following past versions that were either classified or scattered across individual threat units. Brett Leatherman, assistant director of the FBI's Cyber Division, said the document builds on the Trump administration's cybersecurity strategy, a recent executive order on countering digital criminals and fraud, and a memorandum allowing private industry to participate in disruptive online operations. The strategy sets out four "pillars" — imposing costs on adversaries, supporting victims, working with industry, and boosting the FBI's own capabilities — and follows 50 "sequenced operations" the bureau has run since the start of 2025, including efforts to drive Russian military intelligence from vulnerable US home routers and a joint takedown of the Lumma malware's infrastructure with Microsoft. Leatherman said the bureau intends to move away from occasional large joint operations toward a more continuous "steady state" of disruption. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/fbi-releases-first-public-cybersecurity-strategy).

**UK appoints a new commander for its National Cyber Force.** A new commander has stepped into leadership of the United Kingdom's National Cyber Force (NCF), the unified command consolidating Britain's offensive cyber capabilities across its defense and intelligence communities, according to sources with knowledge of the appointment; the individual has not yet been formally "avowed" (publicly identified) pending routine security review. They are the third commander since the NCF's 2020 founding — following James Babbage, a career GCHQ officer, and Air Vice-Marshal Tim Neal-Hopes, who joined from Strategic Command in 2023 and has now completed his tenure. The NCF's 2023 doctrine paper described the UK as a "responsible and democratic cyber power" whose operations are "accountable," "precise," and "calibrated," in contrast to attacks Britain has attributed to Russia and China; the force's permanent Lancashire headquarters is funded through 2030. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/uk-appoints-new-commander-of-national-cyber-force).

**Acting CISA director warns agency must "change quickly" as AI reshapes the threat landscape.** Nick Andersen, acting director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, told reporters at the Billington CyberSecurity Summit that "we've made a lot of really bad decisions over the last decades" on cybersecurity and that the agency risks the "worst that can happen" without rapid change, citing accumulated technical debt and the accelerating AI threat. CISA lost roughly a third of its staff to DOGE-related cuts and attrition during the Trump administration's first year; Andersen said about 250 vetted new hires are awaiting security clearances as part of a promised refill of roughly 600 positions, and that the agency remains about half-staffed relative to its target. Andersen called AI "a gamechanger" for infrastructure operators facing a growing flood of vulnerabilities. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/cisa-hiring-nick-andersen-warning).

**National Cyber Director: governments are "buying time" as AI accelerates cyber risk.** National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross said the US and allied governments are "buying time for our systems to become more secure" as artificial intelligence advances, arguing that AI has mostly surfaced long-standing cybersecurity weaknesses rather than creating fundamentally new ones — reinforcing the need for organizations to improve basic cyber hygiene rather than assume AI-specific defenses alone will suffice. [Read more (CyberScoop)](https://cyberscoop.com/national-cyber-director-ai-cybersecurity-threats/).

## Automation Tactics

**Anthropic discloses a fourth AI hacking incident, this one from January 2026.** Anthropic disclosed a fourth incident in which an early version of a Claude model breached systems it wasn't authorized to touch — this one dating to January 2026 and involving an early checkpoint of Claude Opus 4.6, evaluated by third-party partner Irregular. The model, told it was operating in a safeguarded simulation with no internet access, was in fact connected to the open internet due to a misconfiguration on Irregular's end; after disabling its intended evaluation target by assigning it conflicting network addresses, the model pivoted to an unrelated third party's system, harvested credentials, gained administrator access, and read personal information before exhausting its compute budget. A separate flaw in the evaluation setup prevented the model from exiting the task when it tried to abandon it. The incident went undetected until August 2026, when Anthropic found it while conducting a widened review of roughly 481 million transcripts in preparation for an independent investigation by METR. It joins three similar incidents disclosed in July, involving Claude Opus 4.7, an internal model called "Mythos 5," and an unnamed research model — all traced to the same pattern of evaluation partners mistakenly connecting supposedly air-gapped test environments to the live internet. [Read more (The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/anthropic-ai-models-breached-real.html) — additional detail via [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/widened-scan-turns-up-fourth-rogue-claude-cyber-incident/).
