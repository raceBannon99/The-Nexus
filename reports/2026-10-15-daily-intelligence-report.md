**[Microsoft, Health-ISAC, and Japan's JC3 disrupt a Chinese-language phishing-kit ecosystem](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/microsoft-disrupts-3-phishing-kits.html)** (The Hacker News, primary; corroborated by [The Record](https://therecord.media/microsoft-takes-down-3-major-chinese-phishing-platforms), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/microsoft-disrupts-chinese-language-phishing-kit-ecosystem/), and [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-disrupts-chinese-language-phishing-market-behind-1b-messages/)) — A court-authorized operation seized domains and disabled infrastructure supporting three phishing kits — CoGUI (aka NX/FA), Haozi (aka Phoenix), and Iron Man System — plus the YAM Store marketplace that sold access to compromised Microsoft/Google cloud environments. Microsoft tracks the kit developers as Storm-2990 and the marketplace operators as Storm-3119, describing the ecosystem as Chinese-language but not state-sponsored. Since January 2026 the kits sent over 1 billion phishing emails and SMS messages to 75,000+ organizations across 150+ countries via 17,000+ unique domains; Iron Man System alone held 2,335+ phishing templates spanning 139 countries. *Also covered below under Law Enforcement Disruption.*

**[Brooklyn DA's office disrupts crypto scam targeting Russian community](https://therecord.media/brooklyn-da-server-takedowns-crypto-scam-russian-speakers)** (The Record) — Brooklyn DA Eric Gonzalez's office, working with the FBI, dismantled a fake cryptocurrency-investment scheme that lured Russian-speaking Brooklyn residents through Russian-language Facebook ads. Victims who tried to withdraw funds were locked out and charged fake "fees and taxes," then approached again with bogus recovery services. Authorities seized 241 web domains and 112 servers and recovered roughly $300,000 of what the DA says were "many millions" in losses — part of a nationwide pattern the office puts at $12.7 billion stolen between September 2023 and December 2025. No individual arrests were announced. *Also covered below under Law Enforcement Disruption.*

**[North Macedonian police arrest alleged administrator behind OLVX cybercrime market](https://therecord.media/olvx-cybercrime-marketplace-arrest-north-macedonia)** (The Record, primary; corroborated by [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/olvx-cybercrime-marketplace-operator-charged-north-macedonia/)) — A joint FBI/North Macedonia Ministry of Interior operation led to an arrest (identity withheld) over OLVX, a marketplace that sold stolen personally identifiable information and login credentials through 1,500+ seller accounts, plus phishing kits and remote-desktop-access tools, between 2023 and 2025 — illicit sales reportedly exceeded $500,000. The suspect was actually arrested when the marketplace was disrupted in April 2026 and was formally charged October 14; FBI says the operation endangered "thousands of American citizens." The same suspect allegedly ran a related Telegram group, ONEMILI, offering fraudulent-refund-scheme tools, and authorities say they found evidence of child sexual abuse material on his Telegram account. *Also covered below under Law Enforcement Disruption.*

**[Novel Android RAT "Hirudo" Replaces Banking Apps With Trojanized Copies](https://www.securityweek.com/novel-android-rat-hirudo-replaces-banking-apps-with-trojanized-copies/)** (SecurityWeek; corroborated by Dutch mobile-security firm ThreatFabric, cited at The Hacker News) — Hirudo is a newly disclosed Android banking trojan that replaces genuine banking apps with modified copies carrying a hooking engine that patches out security checks: it returns the app's real signing certificate while running malicious code, hides itself from accessibility-service lists, strips the FLAG_SECURE protection that blocks screen recording, and relays Google Play Integrity checks to attacker-controlled servers that return fake "all clear" verdicts. ThreatFabric found eight Indonesian banking apps specifically trojanized; credential-theft operations tied to the campaign span Indonesia, China, India, Mexico, Morocco, and Thailand. Distribution runs through dropper apps (Shopora, 1V1 X, KoiShort, ZoyaTV) posing as shopping/entertainment apps on fake Google Play pages, promoted via Meta ads. ThreatFabric calls the application-level certificate and integrity-check forgery "a deliberate escalation" beyond prior device-level tampering.

**['Nightmare Scenario': Zero-Click Spyware Is Infecting Phones via Targeted Ads](https://www.wired.com/story/nightmare-scenario-zero-click-spyware-is-infecting-phones-via-targeted-ads/)** (Wired, primary; corroborated by [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/adfection-zero-click-spyware-discover-tech/)) — Citizen Lab disclosed "AdFection," the first publicly documented technical evidence of sophisticated mercenary spyware distributed through real-time-bid digital advertising. An Israeli-founded ad-tech firm, Discover-Tech, ran an ad platform ("Controlium") that Citizen Lab says can de-anonymize, fingerprint, and automatically infect a targeted device the instant one of its ads is served — "someone who gets hacked like this would not see anything amiss," per Citizen Lab senior researcher Bill Marczak. Infected devices contacted Discover-Tech domains, then infrastructure tied to Candiru, an Israeli commercial spyware vendor sanctioned by the US in 2021. Citizen Lab's technical basis is 45 Al Jazeera journalists' devices targeted 2024–2025; by analyzing publicly exposed ad-bidding records, researchers estimate Discover-Tech ads carrying the attacker script ran in 70,000+ apps and 120,000+ websites between March and June 2026, with impressions also observed on NATO and UN networks, EU lawmaking bodies, Australian critical-infrastructure providers, and Norwegian and Swedish armed-forces/government networks. Google terminated Discover-Tech's ad-platform registration after a joint investigation with Citizen Lab and Confiant; IAB Europe suspended the firm from its vendor list. *Also covered below under Government Surveillance.*

**[Cisco Warns of Active Attacks Exploiting New Critical Secure Email Gateway Vulnerability](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisco-warns-of-active-attacks.html)** (The Hacker News, primary; corroborated by [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-warns-of-exploited-secure-email-gateway-vulnerability/) and CISA's KEV catalog) — CVE-2026-76519 (CVSS 9.8) lets an unauthenticated attacker craft a malicious email attachment that writes files to a Cisco Secure Email Gateway appliance's operating system and executes commands as root, when File Reputation/Advanced Malware Protection is enabled with an outdated Content Scanner Tools version. **[Researchers Used a Public OpenAI Model to Build a $250,000 Chrome Full-Chain Exploit in Six Days](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/researchers-used-public-openai-model-to.html)** (The Hacker News) — Security firm XBOW, led by researcher Brendan Dolan-Gavitt, used OpenAI's publicly available GPT-5.6 Sol model to chain three previously unknown Chrome bugs — a V8 JavaScript engine flaw (CVE-2026-85045), an animated-WebP-decoder memory-write flaw, and a Crashpad vulnerability (CVE-2026-78952) — into a full-chain exploit that bypasses both of Chrome's nested sandboxes and executes arbitrary code on Windows from a malicious webpage. **[AI Agents Navigate Unfamiliar OT Systems and Move Equipment in Minutes, Booz Allen Finds](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-agents-navigate-unfamiliar-ot-systems-and-move-equipment-in-minutes-booz-allen-finds/)** (SecurityWeek) — In an isolated OT lab, Booz Allen found frontier AI models could autonomously navigate unfamiliar industrial control systems and manipulate physical equipment, meeting every objective across eight test scenarios with no prior knowledge — no source code, engineering documents, or OT guidance was provided. ## Zero Trust Tactics

**[Noma Security Discloses Claude Code Desktop Exploit](https://devops.com/noma-security-discloses-claude-code-desktop-exploit)** (DevOps.com, via Google Alert) — Noma Security disclosed "SneakPeek," an exploit in Anthropic's Claude Code Desktop app: a preview pane rendering large (256KB+) HTML files as local `file://` URLs in an Electron/Chromium panel granted the rendering process direct local-filesystem access instead of isolating it, letting embedded malicious JavaScript silently exfiltrate credentials, API tokens, and Kubernetes configurations with no user interaction beyond asking Claude Code to review or edit a poisoned file. Anthropic has already shipped a multi-layered fix resolving the identified attack vectors. Noma's Eli Ainhorn: as AI coding tools enter software supply chains largely unvetted by security teams, "it's become apparent that they create a new attack vector that needs to be secured."

**[npm Puts a 48-Hour Clock on Unused Trusted Publishing Configurations](https://devops.com/npm-puts-a-48-hour-clock-on-unused-trusted-publishing-configurations)** (DevOps.com, via Google Alert) — GitHub now expires any npm "trusted publishing" configuration (short-lived OIDC-token-based package publishing, introduced to replace long-lived npm tokens after the Shai-Hulud worm) that doesn't complete a successful release within 48 hours of creation. The change closes a repo-jacking gap: a dormant configuration tied to a repository name that later changes ownership would otherwise still authorize a release from whoever controls that name next — the same class of exposure Aqua Security has estimated affects roughly 3% of sampled GitHub repositories (~9 million projects). Configurations that already published successfully are exempt. npm also now rejects trusted-publishing tokens minted from `issue_comment`-triggered GitHub Actions workflows, joining an existing block on `pull_request_target`.

**[An AI Agent Found a Critical OTP Bypass in Production. The Attack Was the Easy Part.](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/an-ai-agent-found-critical-otp-bypass.html)** (The Hacker News) — Reflectiz's agentic pentesting AI, during an authorized assessment, identified a critical authentication bypass in a major insurance company's one-time-passcode login flow: the server accepted attacker-controlled fields specifying where to deliver a verification code without checking that the destination belonged to the account owner.
