## Adversary Playbook

**[Chinese hacking groups chain two Chrome zero-days and a Windows flaw against NGOs](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/china-linked-hackers-exploit-chrome.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Volexity) — Volexity found two distinct Chinese threat clusters — **UTA0560** and **JungleBamboo** (Chinese-nexus, overlapping the APT31/Violet Typhoon/RedBravo lineage already tracked in the Adversary Tracking Report's China table) — exploiting the same exploit chain against multiple NGOs starting September 1: a Chrome V8 sandbox-escape "patch-gap" flaw (CVE-2026-85046) and a second Chrome sandbox-escape bug (CVE-2026-87491), each previously tracked in this report's Unclear table as unattributed zero-days, now merged into a single attributed China row — plus a Windows ALPC privilege-escalation flaw (CVE-2026-85880). UTA0560 deploys a custom JavaScript backdoor, GRIMWEDGE, via a loader called SUPERSTOMP; JungleBamboo deploys a credential-stealing Chrome extension, LONGTALE/GemStone. The chain exploited a window where Chromium's open-source fixes existed before reaching stable Chrome releases.

**[Red Heron automates Gitea RCE exploitation at scale, hitting 13 organizations in 6 countries](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/red-heron-exploits-gitea-rce-to.html)** (The Hacker News) — A suspected China-linked group researchers call **Red Heron** (moderate confidence, based on Simplified Chinese labels and a Taiwan-focused targeting methodology) turned a public proof-of-concept for CVE-2026-60004, a critical Gitea remote-code-execution flaw, into an automated Python framework ("exp_enhanced.py"), scanning 1,386 Gitea instances across seven countries (477 of them in Taiwan) and confirming compromises at 13 organizations across Taiwan, the US, Canada, Qatar, Argentina, and Sri Lanka — hitting defense, election, energy, aerospace, telecom, government, public-safety, and research targets. The group deployed a 30-plus-command Linux implant, JITTERLY, and a previously undocumented LD_PRELOAD rootkit, SIXZUT. *Editorial note: this CVE was first tracked in the Adversary Tracking Report back on 07-29 and updated 08-26, but no matching row survived into the table's current data — a gap flagged in that report's own Data Quality Notes for Rick's awareness.*

**[Pro-Ukraine "Hacking Cat" group escalates to destructive malware against Russian targets](https://therecord.media/ukraine-malware-russia-ransomware)** (The Record, citing Kaspersky) — The pro-Ukraine hacktivist group **Hacking Cat** has evolved from website defacement and data leaks into destructive attacks, per a new Kaspersky report documenting two previously undocumented malware families: **Gorilla RAT** (a custom remote-access tool deployed via Microsoft Exchange exploitation) and **Monkey Ransomware** (rapidly iterated across multiple language variants — Kaspersky suspects generative AI may be involved). The group has worked alongside Cyber Anarchy Squad (a March breach of a Rosatom contractor) and the Ukrainian Cyber Alliance (a June data-destructive Nemo Wiper attack on Donbassteploenergo, a heating utility in Russian-occupied Donetsk). Hacking Cat disputes some of Kaspersky's tool attributions ("a couple of the tools are ours, sure, but the lockers definitely are not"), and Kaspersky notes that malware-sharing across pro-Ukraine hacktivist groups deliberately complicates individual-group attribution.

**[Microsoft names four splinter groups behind a passkey-phishing and CEO-impersonation fraud wave](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-use-passkey-phishing-to.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Microsoft) — Microsoft is tracking **Storm-3121, Storm-3032, Cordial Spider, and O-UNC-045** — splintered affiliates sharing phishing infrastructure and playbooks — behind two campaigns: AI-generated CEO-impersonation emails tricking finance departments into fraudulent ACH transfers for fake ServiceNow subscriptions (over 1 million phishing emails sent, targeting US IT-services, consumer-goods, real-estate, and manufacturing firms), and IT-support-impersonation vishing directing employees by SMS to counterfeit Microsoft sign-in pages, enabling adversary-in-the-middle credential theft and multi-day cloud-account data exfiltration. Storm-3032 is Microsoft's name for the actor this desk has tracked since 08-10 as UNC6671/"BlackFile" — cross-referenced rather than merged, since this is a materially different campaign from the hedge-fund vishing previously tracked under that name.

**[Compromised HBO Max Reddit account used to push ClickFix malware to macOS and Windows users](https://www.securityweek.com/hacked-hbo-reddit-account-used-for-malware-delivery-via-clickfix-attack/)** (SecurityWeek) — Unnamed attackers, tracked as the **"PasteSwitch"** campaign, compromised the official u/hbomax Reddit account and ran 108 malicious ads across five subreddits promoting a fake native macOS HBO Max app. Clicking through led to a spoofed site (hbomaxx[.]us) with a ClickFix-style terminal-command lure: macOS victims received MacSync/AMOS Helper credential stealers, while Windows victims received Amatera Stealer bundled with cryptocurrency-clipboard-hijacking tools (AnimateClipper, ZigClipper). The underlying blockchain-hosted C2 infrastructure is believed to have been set up more than a year in advance.

**[Revolut discloses fraudsters used a hijacked government email account to obtain customer data](https://therecord.media/revolut-scam-crypto-impersonation)** (The Record; corroborated by [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/personal-financial-info-exposed-in-revolut-data-breach/)) — British fintech Revolut confirmed it disclosed sensitive customer data to fraudsters who submitted fraudulent emergency data requests from a compromised legitimate government email account — circumstantial evidence points to an Italian government domain, unconfirmed. The perpetrators, who have since had their Telegram account suspended, targeted high-net-worth individuals and crypto-asset holders, exposing birth dates, addresses, phone numbers, passport/license copies, verification selfies, bank statements, IBANs, and Bitcoin transaction history, and demanded an extortion payment to avoid releasing the data publicly. The technique directly echoes Lapsus$'s 2021-2022 abuse of compromised law-enforcement accounts for the same kind of fraudulent emergency data request — noted as a technique parallel, not a Lapsus$ attribution.

**[360+ fake government websites target cash-strapped residents across Central Asia](https://therecord.media/hundreds-of-fake-gov-websites-central-asia-scam)** (The Record, citing Russian cybersecurity firm F6) — An unattributed cybercrime operation has built more than 360 fraudulent websites mimicking government portals and regional news outlets across Uzbekistan, Belarus, and Tajikistan, promising cash payments or passive income through fake social-assistance programs. Victims who submit contact details are called by scammers posing as "personal managers" demanding processing fees, or induced to install device-control malware disguised as a registration app, or to submit passport scans enabling further fraud. F6 has not identified the group behind the campaign.

**[Five Black Axe leaders extradited from South Africa on romance-scam charges](https://cyberscoop.com/black-axe-south-africa-leaders-extradited/)** (CyberScoop; corroborated by [The Record](https://therecord.media/)) — Five alleged leaders of the South Africa cell of **Black Axe**, a violent transnational Nigerian criminal organization generating billions annually across dozens of countries, were extradited to the US on Friday: Perry Osagiede, Franklyn Edosa Osagiede, Osariemen Eric Clement, Collins Owhofasa Otughwor, and Musa Mudashiru. The cell ran romance scams and advance-fee fraud from at least 2011 to their 2021 South African arrests — assuming false identities, fabricating emergencies, threatening victims with intimate-image extortion, and laundering proceeds through victims' own accounts. All five face conspiracy to commit wire fraud and money laundering (up to 62 years combined); initial appearances are scheduled in federal court in Trenton, New Jersey. *Also relevant to Law Enforcement Disruption, below.*

**McKesson's stolen data confirmed published after the healthcare supplier refused a $55 million ransom** (N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, [Volume 15, Issue 174](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/174) — the Briefing has no stable per-story permalink, see Sources.md's known limitation; citing a Have I Been Pwned disclosure and The Register) — Extortion group **ShinyHunters**, already tracked in this report since 08-19 for the RingCentral leak and extended 08-31 with a McKesson data-theft claim, has now published the stolen McKesson data after the healthcare supplier refused the group's $55 million ransom demand. Have I Been Pwned's disclosure confirms 6.4 million individuals' names, dates of birth, health records, and contact information were exposed — an escalation from "claimed" to confirmed-published data. *Also relevant to Data Breaches, below.*

**Suspected i-SOON link surfaces for the China-linked UNC3569/GRAYRABBIT campaign** ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/chinese-hackers-exploit-critical-tencent-software-flaw-for-one-click-code-execution/)) — SecurityWeek's fuller writeup on UNC3569's exploitation of the Sogou Input Method flaw (CVE-2026-51990, first tracked here 09-11) adds a suspected connection to Chinese contractor **i-SOON**, based on operational-pattern overlap — a continuation update to the existing row, not a new campaign fact beyond the attribution link.

**[Anthropic CEO's call for an AI slowdown draws Sam Altman's and Elon Musk's public backing — and a pointed non-answer from the Trump administration](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-leaders-are-calling-for-a-slowdown-trumps-team-says-its-on-them)** (Wired, via the Wired Daily newsletter — this story runs under Wired's Business vertical and does not appear in Wired's own Security RSS feed, so it's credited to Gmail per the standing 07-31 convention) — Following Dario Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay (already logged in the Singularity Forecast Report on 09-12/13, and the concrete "6-12 month" capability-threshold claim that drove Wednesday's P5 shift), OpenAI's Sam Altman and Tesla/xAI's Elon Musk both publicly backed Amodei's call for industry restraint over the weekend. The Trump administration's response, per Wired's reporting, is that the decision belongs to the labs themselves — the White House's priority remains maintaining the US's lead over China, not imposing a slowdown.

**[Beijing rejects Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay as a disguised containment strategy](https://www.securityweek.com/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development/)** (SecurityWeek) — China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (spokesperson Guo Jiakun) and the state-run Global Times publicly rejected the framing of Amodei's essay, with the Global Times calling it a "Cold War playbook" rather than a genuine safety concern; China's Commerce Ministry separately rejected US allegations that Chinese AI firms are "distilling" capabilities from American frontier models (the joint NSA/CISA/FBI advisory already tracked in the Adversary Tracking Report's China table), calling distillation standard industry practice. Xi Jinping continues to push a separate Global-South AI-governance framework rather than joining the evaluator-access measure OpenAI and Anthropic adopted this week. *Also relevant to Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law, below.*

**No range change today, but not because these two stories didn't matter — they land on the same two forces Wednesday's P5 shift already weighed against each other, not a new axis.** Wednesday's shift rested on one concrete fact (Amodei's 6-12-month claim) tempered by one countervailing fact (labs voluntarily adopting the essay's own outside-evaluator mitigation, which is why the shift was one year, not more). Altman's and Musk's public backing strengthens that tempering side — more lab-CEO buy-in on the mitigation, not a new capability data point. Beijing's rejection strengthens the concern side, but it isn't new information about the coordination gap itself — Wednesday's own reasoning already flagged "government-coordination asks... less likely to be enacted quickly" as a known limitation. What it does add: China's dismissal of the distillation concern specifically corroborates a Sooner data point already logged 09-09 — capability diffusing to state-linked labs faster than containment assumes — by confirming that concern isn't being addressed diplomatically, only adversarially via detection. The two roughly cancel out rather than compound, so the range holds at P5 2027 · P50 2042 · P95 2100. Worth watching for a genuine new case in either direction: Beijing explicitly declining any monitoring commitment going forward (Sooner), or the evaluator-access measure actually catching something reportable (Later). Full evidence → anchor → adjustment reasoning in the Singularity Forecast Report's Evidence Log.

**[Microsoft publishes a draft "Humanist AI Code of Conduct" barring its models from producing offensive cyber tooling](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-ai-code-of-conduct-sets-cyberattack-boundaries-chain-of-command-safety-constraints/)** (SecurityWeek) — Microsoft published a draft code of conduct for its MAI models: an absolute prohibition on producing working exploit code, attack tooling, or targeting methodology regardless of how a request is framed (defensive vulnerability research remains permitted); an explicit chain-of-command hierarchy in which tool outputs, file contents, and messages from other AI systems carry no inherent authority over model behavior; agent constraints requiring minimum-privilege operation with no self-directed scope expansion or self-escalation of permissions; and a transparency requirement barring obscured chain-of-thought reasoning. The code is currently a draft, subject to a six-week public consultation, targeting 2027 model development — no shipped model has been trained on it yet. Logged in the Singularity Forecast Report as a modest Later signal: a third leading lab, after OpenAI's and Anthropic's evaluator-access commitments logged this week, adopting formal pre-deployment safety constraints — though prospective rather than an immediate operational change, so it doesn't independently move the range either. *Also relevant to Automation Tactics, below.*

## Law Enforcement Disruption

**[Manhattan DA seizes a dozen celebrity deepfake websites in the largest takedown of its kind](https://www.wired.com/story/new-york-seizes-a-dozen-celebrity-deepfake-websites/)** (Wired) — The Manhattan District Attorney's Office seized 12 domains used to unlawfully share, publish, and sell nonconsensual celebrity deepfake pornography, in what Wired reports is likely the largest such takedown to date. Roughly 1,200 people — overwhelmingly women, including social-media influencers, actors, activists, athletes, musicians, and politicians (56 European politicians identified by one researcher, including 16 in the US and 2 in the UK) — were depicted on the sites, some active since 2018 and viewed millions of times. DA Alvin Bragg's office has not named the sites' operators; its investigation into who is responsible is ongoing. The seizures were carried out under New York's criminal-procedure laws, building on enforcement pathways the federal Take It Down Act has opened up.

## Cybersecurity Canon Project Book Reviews (July 2026)

**["Cyber Citizens: Saving Democracy with Digital Literacy" by Heidi Boghosian](https://cybercanon.org/cyber-citizens-saving-democracy-with-digital-literacy/)** — Reviewer William Jay Carson calls this an "interesting read" with strong sections on privacy and the cyber-activist community, but flags what he sees as an anti-conservative slant coloring the general-knowledge sections and several factual claims he couldn't corroborate (US voter turnout, JROTC characterization, an F.E. Warren AFB "toxic" description). Not recommended for the Hall of Fame; recommended as a decent niche read for readers interested in privacy/GDPR topics specifically, alongside Daniel Solove's "On Privacy and Technology" and Anu Bradford's "Digital Empires."

**["The Browser Security Field Manual" by Audrey Adeline and Vivek Ramachandran](https://cybercanon.org/the-browser-security-field-manual/)** — Reviewer Sandip Dholakia recommends this book for the Hall of Fame, calling it a timely, practical treatment of the browser as "the new enterprise endpoint" — covering adaptive phishing, malicious extensions, session hijacking, OAuth abuse, and data exfiltration that never touches the traditional endpoint. The authors (a SquareX security researcher and its founder) organize threats into clear categories rather than isolated techniques, though the book assumes familiarity with OAuth flows, tokens, and browser APIs and isn't an entry-level read.

## Zero Trust Tactics

**[Critical, actively exploited Cisco Secure Email Gateway flaw added to CISA's KEV catalog](https://www.securityweek.com/root-rce-zero-day-in-cisco-secure-email-gateway-under-active-exploitation/)** (SecurityWeek; also covered by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisco-secure-email-gateway-flaw.html); primary-source advisory: [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/14/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog)) — CVE-2026-76461, a critical (CVSS 9.8) vulnerability in AsyncOS Software for Cisco Secure Email Gateway, allows unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary commands with root privileges via crafted email messages exploiting an SQL-injection flaw in email-parsing logic. Cisco confirmed active exploitation but has not disclosed scale or named an exploiting actor. CISA added the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, giving federal agencies until September 17 to patch. No adversary or campaign is attached to this disclosure, so it does not get an Adversary Tracking Report row per that table's own no-identifiable-campaign rule.
