## Adversary Playbook

**Handala Hack's persona and the CHOSEN BRICK spyware campaign are confirmed to be the same Iranian actor, Void Manticore.** Group-IB research, corroborated by the UK National Cyber Security Centre's independent tracking of the same tooling, confirms that Void Manticore — an Iran-linked, MOIS-affiliated actor — operates both the "Handala Hack" hacktivist persona (already tracked here for the Stryker Intune-wiper attack) and CHOSEN BRICK, the Windows spyware implant this desk has tracked since September 16 targeting UK/US/Netherlands-based dissidents and journalists. Group-IB documents the CHOSEN BRICK tool under the name HEAVYGRAM, a Python-based Telegram backdoor active since September 2023 offering remote command execution, system/network/process discovery, data and Telegram-session exfiltration, screenshot capture, and password theft via Windows Registry autorun persistence. A companion Delphi staging tool, CRUDEEXCLUDE (first documented by Google in December 2025), masquerades as legitimate apps like Pictory and Telegram to prepare systems for HEAVYGRAM deployment. HEAVYGRAM has separately been used against Iranian dissidents, opposition journalists, and activist groups, including a Canada-documented operation doxxing Iran International journalists in September 2025. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/iran-linked-handala-hack-tied-to.html)

**A second-stage Brevo supply-chain attack injected malware into 100,000+ websites.** Cybersecurity firm Sansec disclosed that the attacker behind Brevo's September 10 email-marketing breach (already tracked here) returned September 14 with a stolen Cloudflare API key, deploying a malicious Cloudflare Worker across Brevo-connected sites. The worker injected fake Cloudflare-verification pages using the ClickFix social-engineering technique — tricking visitors into pasting and running attacker commands — and attempted to install malicious WordPress plugins on sites where an administrator was already logged in. The campaign affected more than 100,000 websites and remained active for roughly 4 to 5.5 hours before Brevo removed the malicious worker. No actor has been named. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/brevo-supply-chain-attack-injects-malware-into-100000-websites/)

**Revolut's extortionist identified as "IAmNotAVillain"; actor demands $3 million.** Threat-intelligence firm Hudson Rock has traced the actor behind Revolut's fraudulent-emergency-data-request breach (disclosed by Revolut over the weekend of September 12-13) to a persona operating as "IAmNotAVillain," who initially compromised Italian government employee accounts using infostealer malware, then used a compromised Italian Ministry of the Interior employee's email address to impersonate official government authority and submit fraudulent data requests to Revolut. The actor has publicly demanded $3 million from Revolut, though the company says it has received no direct contact or demand from the individuals or group making the claims. Roughly 680 Revolut customers — described as cryptocurrency-asset holders — had passports, addresses, phone numbers, verification selfies, bank statements, and transaction history exposed; separately, more than 147GB of data was stolen from Italian law enforcement in the same scheme. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/revolut-data-breach-5-months-680-high-profile-accounts-3m-ransom/)

**PhantomRaven's operator identified as an individual bug-bounty fraudster using LLM-written malware.** CrowdStrike has attributed PhantomRaven — the LLM-generated npm infostealer this desk began tracking September 17 — to a single, financially motivated individual operating under multiple aliases (jpd12, jpd13, npmhell, npmpackagejpd, and others) and two npm publisher accounts (jpdhellonpm1, jpd15). CrowdStrike assesses with high confidence that the malware itself was LLM-authored, based on verbose comments, placeholder code, and statistical token-analysis patterns. The operator has claimed bug-bounty rewards from at least nine entities across technology, retail, and hospitality sectors since November 2022, using the stolen credentials and CI/CD environment variables solely to identify and falsely claim rewards rather than sell data on stealer marketplaces — a motive pattern CrowdStrike says is consistent with the absence of any stealer-marketplace listings for the stolen data. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/claimed-bug-bounty-hunter-likely-used.html)

**WeaselBiscuit infostealer spreads via 13 npm packages, hedged DPRK tradecraft overlap.** Researchers at OpenSourceMalware (Paul McCarty and Jenn Gile) documented WeaselBiscuit, a cross-platform (Windows/macOS/Linux) JavaScript infostealer distributed through 13 malicious npm packages, including "@biz44/id10-client," "process-lhpm," and "engin1." The malware harvests Chrome-extension storage — including wallet-extension state — and can log clipboard contents and keystrokes on Windows. The researchers note "meaningful overlap" with DPRK-associated Contagious Interview tooling, based on shared infrastructure patterns like Npoint.io-style command-and-control, but stop short of a formal attribution. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/weaselbiscuit-stealer-spreads-via-13.html)

**RatHat Android malware abuses ADB for persistent access, hedged to "China-based" actors.** Zimperium researchers Gianluca Braga, Vishnu Pratapagiri, and Fernando Ortega documented RatHat, AI-powered Android malware that abuses accessibility services and the Android Debug Bridge to escape the app sandbox, harvest credentials, record screens, intercept SMS messages, and retain shell-level access to local daemons even after the app is uninstalled. It is distributed via smishing and malvertising through deceptive third-party download portals. Zimperium attributes the malware generally to "China-based threat actors," without naming a specific group. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/rathat-android-malware-abuses-adb-to.html)

**Mass exploitation of an AI-agent orchestration framework, Orkes Conductor.** Security firms Empirical Security and Fortinet report ongoing exploitation of CVE-2026-58138 (CVSS 9.8), an unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw in Orkes Conductor, an open-source framework for orchestrating microservices, workflows, and AI agents. Attackers inject malicious JavaScript or Python expressions into inline workflow definitions sent to the platform's API; a "HostAccess.ALL" configuration that disables sandboxing lets the injected code execute OS commands as the Conductor process, which often runs with root privileges. The flaw was patched in version 3.30.2 in June, proof-of-concept code was published in early August, and exploitation began around August 21; Fortinet says it blocked roughly 1,300 exploitation attempts between September 8 and 9. No actor has been named. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/critical-orkes-conductor-vulnerability-exploited-in-attacks/)

**Israeli contractor BlackCore trained Angolan officials to run their own influence operations.** Research from the University of Toronto's Citizen Lab shows BlackCore, an Israeli "influence-for-hire" firm, ran a 14-week program training Angolan government officials to conduct online influence operations themselves — including creating fake social-media personas and content promoting the government through a fictitious media outlet, "Agita News." Participants produced more than 40 pieces of content; some deceptive Facebook posts approached 50,000 likes. Citizen Lab found no evidence Israel's government commissioned or funded the work — this is a commercial client relationship, not a state-directed campaign. France's foreign-interference watchdog Viginum separately linked BlackCore to a 2026 information operation against the France Unbowed party during municipal elections, and suspects the firm in operations targeting New York, Scotland, and Togo; a related Israel-origin influence network Meta disrupted in an August 2026 threat report is, per Citizen Lab, highly likely BlackCore's work. [The Record](https://therecord.media/angola-israel-influence-operations-blackcore)

**Hacktivist group CikLeak claims breach of Russia's Central Election Commission ahead of the State Duma vote.** A group calling itself CikLeak says it broke into computer systems belonging to Russia's Central Election Commission and companies developing Vybory, the state election-administration platform — including telecom giant Rostelecom — days before Russia's September 18-20 parliamentary elections. The group claims to have stolen internal documents, server configurations, passwords, and employee communications, and provided the material to independent investigative outlet Important Stories, which said it authenticated the documents. CikLeak says it did not intend to disrupt voting, framing the leak instead as exposing the election system's internal workings and opportunities for manipulation; it urged Russians to vote in person on the final day, arguing that would make manipulation more difficult. Russian election officials had warned of rising cyberattacks against election infrastructure in the run-up to the vote, and CEC Chair Ella Pamfilova said the intensity, volume, and speed of recent attacks was difficult to compare with anything seen before. No independent group has verified CikLeak's claims beyond Important Stories' document authentication. [The Record](https://therecord.media/russia-election-hackers-breach)

## Zero Trust Tactics

**Rogue external MFA providers can turn Microsoft Entra sign-in flows into credential traps.** Researchers at Varonis documented "TrustSink," a technique that abuses trusted external authentication providers to intercept passwords. Demonstrated against Microsoft Entra — though the researchers say it could work with any provider — the technique requires an attacker to already hold sufficient privileges within a victim's cloud identity environment. Once inside, the attacker places a credential-harvesting login page inside the legitimate sign-in flow: "Entra trusts a signed token from a registered [external authentication method] without verifying what the provider shows the user," letting the attacker control what the user sees while Entra automatically validates whatever comes back, persisting through password resets. [N2K Cyberwire, Sep. 17 Daily Briefing](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/178)

**A critical Check Point management-server flaw allows unauthenticated root access.** Check Point disclosed CVE-2026-91843 (CVSS 9.8), a stack overflow in its Security Management and Log Servers' login process triggered by a login request with an extremely long username, allowing unauthenticated remote code execution as root. Check Point says it has no indication of exploitation in the wild, and CISA's own assessment records exploitation as "none." No discoverer or actor was disclosed. The flaw is the fifth critical, unauthenticated management-server vulnerability in Check Point products since July 2026; roughly 3,836 hosts globally still present default Check Point management identities. A LivePatch fix is available for supported versions. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/critical-check-point-management-server.html)

**A re-registered, abandoned CDN domain leaves thousands of sites pointed at a stranger's infrastructure.** Researchers describe a CDN domain, abandoned and re-registered in July 2025, whose new owner now controls wildcard DNS across the entire domain — meaning any hardcoded hostname beneath it resolves to infrastructure the new owner controls. Thousands of websites, code repositories, and documentation pages still reference hostnames under the domain. The pattern echoes the 2024 polyfill.io incident, in which a JavaScript shim embedded in more than 110,000 sites changed ownership and began serving conditional redirects; the current case has not been linked to confirmed active exploitation, but the researchers cite a September 2026 incident in which compromised e-commerce sites ran social-engineering campaigns as evidence the pattern is already being exploited elsewhere. No actor has been named in this specific case. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/an-abandoned-cdn-domain-was-re.html)

**White House shelves AI oversight-agency framework after industry lobbying; Trump calls AI safety concerns a "hoax."** WIRED's Hugo Lowell reports that White House aides had begun sketching a FINRA-style AI oversight body — an idea floated by Google DeepMind cofounder Demis Hassabis in a July essay, drafted jointly by Treasury and the Office of Science and Technology Policy — before tech executives including former White House AI czar David Sacks and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg individually called President Trump in August to object. No new AI oversight proposal has been seriously considered at the White House since. Trump has separately called AI safety concerns a "hoax" in a Truth Social post; aides say his irritation was fueled partly by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay (tracked here since September 14), which the administration viewed as stirring unnecessary anxiety despite representing a moderate position within AI-safety circles. In Congress, the bipartisan FRONTIER Act — which would let Commerce Department auditors embedded at AI labs report safety incidents and halt development of catastrophically risky models, and has public backing from OpenAI — has no path to a floor vote under Speaker Mike Johnson before the midterms; House Democrats are exploring attaching its audit provisions to a must-pass continuing resolution instead. Two competing "kill switch" bills, from Rep. Ted Lieu and Sen. John Kennedy, draw only modest interest from tech executives, who note a kill switch does little good against harms — like the Hugging Face breach — not discovered until after the fact. "So for now," Lowell writes, "even as conversations about AI models killing us all have taken over the airwaves, it doesn't really look like the US government is going to do anything about it. At least until events force everyone's hand." [Wired, via Gmail](https://www.wired.com/story/washington-wont-be-regulating-ai-anytime-soon/)

**Op-ed: named researchers and DHS/CISA officials push back on "AI hacking apocalypse" framing.** A CyberScoop op-ed gathers a cluster of security researchers and two federal officials arguing that catastrophic AI-driven cyberattack scenarios are technically implausible at current capability levels and preventable through existing security practices. Juan Andres Guerrero-Saade argues "doomer" narratives misuse cybersecurity concerns to fuel unfounded apocalypse framing; Matt Tait notes frontier models require datacenter-scale compute that only exists in centralized facilities, making independent, rogue deployment implausible; Ciaran Martin dismisses warnings about AI-orchestrated botnets taking over the internet as lacking a credible technical mechanism. DHS's Joseph Alm and other officials frame the risk as manageable through pre-release testing and "good sandboxes," while researcher Matt Hartman calls for monitoring agent activity, constraining permissions, and building stronger operational safeguards rather than AI-specific new approaches. The piece references OpenAI's and Anthropic's own disclosed rogue-agent incidents but frames them as evidence of operational negligence, not inherent AI danger. [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-agent-hacking-apocalypse-cybersecurity/)

## Cybersecurity Automation Tactics

**Two disclosed flaws let attackers turn AI coding agents against their own sandboxes.** Security firm Air Security disclosed "Plugin4Shell," a flaw in how AI coding agents verify pinned plugin versions: agents lock plugins to reviewed code via commit hashes but never verify the fetched code actually matches that hash, letting a malicious repository owner create branch names resembling commit hashes to redirect installations to different code while the agent falsely reports installing the locked version. The flaw affects Anthropic's Claude Code (patched in v2.1.179), OpenAI's Codex (patched in v0.146.0), GitHub Copilot (no fix available), and Google's Gemini CLI (will not be patched; being retired) — though exposure is limited mainly to non-GitHub repositories, since GitHub itself disallows branch names resembling commit hashes. No exploitation has been observed. Separately, researchers Oren Yomtov and Jurre van Bergen disclosed two critical flaws (CVE-2026-77179, CVE-2026-79994) in Docker Sandboxes' isolation between virtualized AI-agent environments and macOS hosts, letting malicious code running inside an agent's sandbox read or modify files anywhere on the host, or reach Unix domain sockets outside the workspace — relevant specifically because agents inside these sandboxes routinely install packages and run commands with elevated privileges. Neither flaw has been exploited in the wild or added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. [The Hacker News: Plugin4Shell](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/plugin4shell-lets-repository-owners.html) · [The Hacker News: Docker Sandboxes](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/critical-docker-sandboxes-flaw-lets.html)

## Critical Infrastructure Attacks

**CISA issues its first guidance on deceiving attackers with decoys.** The agency published a 22-page document, "Using Cyber Decoys to Strengthen Detection and Response," its first formal guidance on deploying honeypots and honeytokens to detect and disrupt intruders. Chris Butera, acting executive director of CISA's cybersecurity division, said decoys can be "a very low-cost but actually high-fidelity way to detect an adversary," and the guidance is aimed specifically at critical-infrastructure owners and operators who "don't have the most personnel or money" — noting decoys pair well with zero-trust and assume-compromise strategies. The guidance defines honeytokens as data elements or logical objects with no legitimate business use (fake records, credentials, or files) designed to trigger alerts on unauthorized access. [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/cisa-guidance-cyber-decoys-critical-infrastructure/)

## Cybersecurity Risk Forecasting Tactics

**Ransomware attacks on manufacturers surged 40% as supply-chain risk grows.** Black Kite's 2026 Manufacturing & Distribution Ransomware Report, analyzing disclosed victims from January 2023 through July 2026, found ransomware attacks on manufacturers rose 40% in the first seven months of 2026 compared with the same period in 2025, with 1,183 new incidents recorded. The UK's Cyber Monitoring Centre estimated a £1.9 billion financial impact from the Jaguar Land Rover attack alone. European attacks surged 85% year-over-year while US attacks held roughly flat; the US was targeted 412 times (35% of the global total), Europe 369 times, and the rest of the world 402 times. Notably, half of 2026's attacks came from ransomware groups that didn't exist two years ago. Named groups include The Gentlemen (12% of 2026 manufacturing attacks, 142 claimed victims by mid-year — already tracked in this report's Adversary Tracking table) and Qilin (also already tracked), alongside Akira, DragonForce, INC Ransom, SafePay (22% of 2025 German attacks), and Clop. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/ransomware-attacks-on-manufacturers-surge-as-supply-chain-risk-grows/)

## Cybersecurity Workforce Development Tactics

**Congress weighs new mental-health support for US Cyber Command after a cluster of suicide deaths.** Congressional sources describe a recent cluster of suicide deaths among US Cyber Command personnel and civilian staff as an inflection point, coming as the Pentagon's appetite for cyber capabilities grows following operations against Iran and Venezuela — a pattern some compare to the toll drone pilots experienced early in the Global War on Terrorism. The most likely legislative vehicle is the annual National Defense Authorization Act; Rep. Sarah Elfreth (D-MD), whose district abuts Fort Meade, intends to push for expanded access to security-cleared mental-health providers and a redefinition of how the Pentagon counts a "suicide cluster" to include civilian employees. A 2024 NDAA-mandated study found existing resources for Cyber Mission Force personnel "inadequate" and posing risk to mission effectiveness, though no implementation plan has been delivered to Congress. Cyber Command's own leadership has asked service cyber-branch chiefs to check in weekly on suicide-prevention efforts and held mandatory counseling for affected units; a former Army Cyber Command officer, Andrew Schoka, said burnout is "a consequence of the extreme operational tempo" of teams that are "essentially 'deployed-in-place' continually without defined rest and reset cycles," calling added mental-health resources important but insufficient without addressing under-qualified technical leadership. The NSA has experienced related recent suicides; Deputy Director Tim Kosiba was called to the White House last month to discuss the matter. [The Record](https://therecord.media/congress-eyes-support-for-cyber-command-suicide-deaths)

## Compliance Trends

**EU proposes barring children under 13 from social media, mandating age verification.** The European Commission laid out a roadmap for the EU KIDS Act, which would bar children younger than 13 from social-media accounts entirely (outside parent-controlled tools) and set a bloc-wide minimum age of 15 for account creation, with 13-15 year-olds limited to guardian-created "mini accounts" capping screen time at one hour daily. Platforms would be required to prove products are "safe by design" — banning addictive features, infinite scroll without stopping points, and AI chatbots/companions that simulate emotional-dependency-creating relationships with minors, which must be off by default. Enforcement would use an EU-built age-verification app (unveiled in April, which the Commission says stores no identity documents or biometric data) alongside account-creation-date and payment-data-based age estimation for existing accounts; violators face fines up to 6% of global annual sales. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said today's children engage with "the most sophisticated technologies ever created... never created with their wellbeing in mind." Critics, including CCIA Europe and European Digital Rights, warn the proposal defers key technical and accreditation standards to future rulemaking and could force adults to submit ID documents to use mainstream platforms at all — with particular harm to people who lack formal identification. [The Record](https://therecord.media/european-commission-set-to-push-social-media-kids-restrictions-into-law)

## Cybersecurity Framework Trends

**CISA retires its weekly vulnerability bulletin for a risk-based approach.** CISA is discontinuing its weekly vulnerability bulletin — a comprehensive, alphabetically sorted list of new vulnerabilities with no prioritization guidance — effective September 28, in favor of Binding Operational Directive 26-04, issued in June, which requires federal agencies to prioritize vulnerabilities based on real-world risk factors like evidence of exploitation and exposure rather than severity scores alone. CISA says the weekly bulletin had contributed to alert fatigue without providing threat context, and will continue surfacing vulnerability information through its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, alerts, and advisories instead — sources that already focus on actively exploited flaws rather than comprehensive weekly counts. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cisa-retires-weekly-vulnerability-bulletin-in-risk-based-pivot/)
