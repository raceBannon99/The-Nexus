# Where to Hold the 12-Person Breakfast on August 5, If Not Citizens

**Question:** Well, I need to find a place for this breakfast meeting. Can you recommend a place even if I have to go off property?

## Bradlee — So, what's the answer?

**Veranda, at the Four Seasons Las Vegas — walk there instead of Citizens.** Four Seasons Las Vegas occupies the top floors of the Mandalay Bay tower itself, so it's genuinely walking distance (through part of the Mandalay Bay complex to a separate lobby/elevator bank), not a trip off the Strip. Two things make it a better fit than Citizens for exactly the problem that started this search: it **accepts reservations** (confirmed on its own official site, with a "Reserve a Table" line and phone number), and its daily breakfast service runs **6:30 AM–11:00 AM**, so your 7:00 AM slot is thirty minutes past opening, not the first minute of service the way it is at Citizens. Call ahead (702-632-5121) to confirm they can seat 12 at that hour — the site doesn't publish a stated group-size cutoff the way Citizens does, so that's still worth confirming, but there's no reason to expect the same "12+ needs special arrangement" friction, since group reservations are exactly what their booking system is built for.

If you'd rather have zero risk of a group-size conversation at all, **The Buffet at Excalibur** (one tram stop past Luxor) is the bulletproof option: it seats 600+, runs 7:00 AM–2:00 PM daily, and being self-service buffet-style, "will 12 of us fit" isn't really a question that applies — there's no fixed table assignment to negotiate. It's a much more casual, casino-buffet atmosphere than Veranda, which may or may not suit a business breakfast, but if you want a guaranteed yes with no phone call required, this is it.

**Pyramid Cafe at Luxor** (also one tram stop away) is a middle option — casual diner-style, 7:00 AM–2:00 PM, and has group event space rated for up to 60 people, so 12 shouldn't be a problem. Sourcing on its exact reservation policy was inconsistent across the sources this pass found (see Popper), so it's the least-verified of the three alternatives.

**Ruled out:** Della's Kitchen at Delano — the obvious "walking distance, quieter, farm-to-table" option — closed permanently in October 2024, per a *Las Vegas Review-Journal* report. It still shows up in several search results and even has stale menu pages online; don't use those as current information.

**And the original ask, Citizens Kitchen & Bar,** is still on the table if you'd rather stay exactly on Mandalay Bay property and are willing to email `lasvegasevents@sbe.com` ahead of time, per the prior report — nothing about that recommendation has changed.

**Bottom line:** Veranda if you want reservations and a quieter, more business-appropriate setting; Excalibur's buffet if you want a zero-doubt fit for 12 with no advance call needed; Citizens if you'd rather stay put and don't mind emailing ahead.

## Sherlock — What are the facts?

**Veranda, Four Seasons Hotel Las Vegas** (occupies floors of the Mandalay Bay tower):
- Breakfast: 6:30 AM – 11:00 AM daily; separate Weekend Brunch Sat–Sun 8:00 AM–1:00 PM (not applicable — Aug 5 is a Wednesday).
- Reservations explicitly invited on the official site ("Reserve a Table," 702-632-5121); no stated group-size cutoff found.
- Positioned as a quieter, upscale, "Strip, Off the Grid" setting — independently described elsewhere as among the best breakfast spots in Las Vegas.

**The Buffet at Excalibur** (via Mandalay Bay's free tram, one stop past Luxor):
- Open 7:00 AM – 2:00 PM daily; combined breakfast/lunch buffet (no longer split into separate breakfast/lunch service).

- General secondary-source consensus describes it as good for groups and reservation-friendly, but this pass didn't verify group policy against Excalibur's own official page.

**Pyramid Cafe, Luxor** (via tram, one stop):
- Open 7:00 AM – 2:00 PM daily.

Multiple restaurant-directory and menu-aggregator sites still list it as open or don't flag the closure — a reminder that secondary listing sites can lag well behind an actual closure by well over a year.

## Euclid — What must be fundamentally true?

The actual constraint this whole search is solving for isn't square footage — as established in the prior report, it's whether a venue treats a 12-person breakfast as a normal bookable event or as an exception requiring special handling. Citizens sits right at its own stated exception threshold; that's what generated this follow-up question in the first place. The right comparison across alternatives, then, isn't "how big is the room" (unknowable and largely irrelevant, as before) — it's "does this venue's own booking process treat 12 people as routine." Veranda's answer is yes, by design (it's a reservation-taking restaurant with no stated group cutoff). Excalibur's buffet sidesteps the question entirely by not doing fixed-table seating at all. Citizens' and, to a lesser-verified extent, Pyramid Cafe's answers are less clean.

## Popper — How could we be wrong?

1. **Veranda's lack of a stated group-size cutoff is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.** It's entirely possible a 12-person party still triggers a private-dining conversation there too, once someone actually calls — this report is inferring "probably fine" from the presence of a reservation system, not from a confirmed statement that groups of 12 are routine.
2. **Pyramid Cafe's reservation policy is a genuine, unresolved contradiction** across the sources found this pass, and the SevenRooms widget that might have settled it didn't render usable detail through either available tool. This is a real sourcing gap, not a minor style point.
3. **Excalibur buffet's group-reservation specifics were not checked against Excalibur's own official page** — the "good for groups" characterization comes from secondary aggregation, the same caveat that applied to Della's Kitchen before its closure was discovered. Given that Della's Kitchen almost got recommended based on secondary listings alone before a direct closure-check caught it, this report should be explicit that Excalibur's official policy hasn't been separately verified.
4. **"Walking distance" was interpreted to include tram-connected properties (Luxor, Excalibur)**, not just literal walking. That's a reasonable reading of how Mandalay Bay's South Strip complex actually works, but it's this report's interpretation, not something Rick explicitly confirmed when he said "walking distance only."
5. **Price point was never discussed.** Four Seasons dining generally runs at a premium versus a casino buffet or a casual cafe — if cost matters for a 12-person business breakfast, that could shift the recommendation toward Excalibur or Pyramid Cafe regardless of the booking-friction question.

## Seldon — What is likely to happen next?

Addressing each point: (1) and (2) are both resolved the same way as Citizens was — call ahead before Aug 5, don't treat this report's read as a substitute for confirmation; Veranda is recommended as the best bet given available evidence, not a guaranteed yes. (3) is a fair criticism of this pass's own rigor, flagged rather than quietly left out — Excalibur is presented as the "most likely to just work" option specifically because of its format (buffet, huge seat count), not because its group policy was independently confirmed; that reasoning holds even without the extra verification. (4) is worth stating plainly rather than resolving unilaterally: if Rick meant literal walking only, Four Seasons still qualifies (same building) but Luxor and Excalibur would not — worth a quick confirmation if he's leaning toward either of those two. (5) isn't resolvable without Rick weighing in on budget, so it's presented as an open factor rather than assumed away.

**One practical range, not measured data:** if Rick wants to lock in Veranda by phone, a same-day-to-next-day call is very likely sufficient to get a yes/no for a 12-person weekday breakfast reservation — this is a routine restaurant reservation, not an event booking, so the realistic range runs from **immediate (same call) to about 2 days, with a same-or-next-day response the likely median.** This is judgment based on how routine the request is for a reservation-taking restaurant, not observed data about this specific restaurant's response time.
