# What Frontier AI's Leaders Say About Superintelligence and the Singularity

**Question:** Using the "Who's Who in Frontier AI" diagram from the artifacts library (`images/whos-who-in-frontier-ai.png`), find whether the same thought leaders and CEOs have made predictions for when the AI industry reaches the Superintelligence and the Singularity milestones, and build a range of predictions from the answers — the same forecasting analysis already run for the AGI milestone (`reports/2026-09-19-agi-ceo-predictions.md`).

Roughly the same handful of voices who spoke on AGI also spoke on Superintelligence — Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, Demis Hassabis, Alexandr Wang, Elon Musk, and Jared Kaplan all named a superintelligence-adjacent date or window, clustering earlier than most outside observers assume: 2027 to roughly 2031, barely a few years past their own AGI estimates. Musk's claim is the most extreme — that AI will exceed *all of humanity's combined intelligence*, not just the smartest individual human, by around 2031. On the Singularity, the roster goes nearly silent on dates but loud on direction: within the last two weeks, Amodei, Altman, Hassabis, and (separately) Musk have all gone on record warning that humanity could lose meaningful control of frontier AI — Musk is the only one who attached a specific year (2036) and a number (10–20% chance of human extinction) to that warning. Both readings sit inside Nexus's own standing ranges (Superintelligence: P5 2029 · P50 2045 · P95 2100; Singularity: P5 2027 · P50 2042 · P95 2100) rather than ahead of them, and for the same reason the AGI survey concluded what it did: every aggressive voice here has a commercial or reputational stake in the capability story, and the recent flurry of *loss-of-control* warnings is itself already priced into the Singularity Forecast Report's current range via the daily-report pipeline's own tracking of Amodei's September 12 "Pace the Frontier" essay and its fallout.

Two structural findings carry as much weight as the dates. First, the industry's own language is far more disciplined about *Superintelligence* than about *the Singularity*: multiple roster CEOs will name a year for superintelligence, but almost none will name a year for loss of control — the Singularity is discussed by this roster almost entirely as a risk to manage, not a date to predict, and Musk's willingness to name 2036 is the outlier, not the norm. Second, one statement in this roster is genuinely ambiguous between AGI and Superintelligence by Nexus's own definitional standard: Amodei's "Powerful AI" description — a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" outperforming human experts at almost all cognitive tasks — was treated as this roster's most credible near-term AGI claim in the prior report, but its own wording arguably clears Soares' harder Superintelligence bar (beats the single best human at every cognitive task) rather than just the AGI bar. That tension is not resolved by picking a side; it's recorded as a standing caveat on both files.

## The Facts (Sherlock)

Research covered the same roster as the AGI engagement, this time searching specifically for superintelligence-timing and loss-of-control statements. ### Superintelligence

**Sam Altman (OpenAI).** In a September 2024 essay, "The Intelligence Age," Altman wrote that superintelligence could be achievable "in a few thousand days" — roughly 2027–2031. Separately, at an AI event in India, he was reported to have suggested superintelligence could arrive by the end of 2028 (secondary reporting of a public remark, not a direct quote verified against a transcript in this pass). His June 2025 essay "The Gentle Singularity" reframes the transition as already underway and gradual ("we are past the event horizon; the takeoff has started") rather than naming a fresh date — treated here as framing, not an additional data point.

**Dario Amodei (Anthropic).** Described "Powerful AI" — a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" outperforming human experts at almost all cognitive tasks — as achievable "as soon as late 2026 or early 2027." **Definitional flag, carried from the AGI report:** this description was used there as Amodei's AGI claim; read strictly against Soares' Superintelligence bar (beats the single best human at every cognitive task), the same wording arguably clears the higher bar instead. Recorded here as the same statement, same date, flagged as ambiguous between the two milestones rather than double-counted as independent evidence for both.

**Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind).** Stated superintelligence is "at best, a few years out," while cautioning "no one really knows" — vague relative to his own more specific AGI estimate (~2029–2030), but directionally implies the low 2030s if "a few years" is read as following AGI arrival rather than naming an independent date.

**Alexandr Wang (Meta Superintelligence Labs).** At the India AI Impact Summit (reported February–March 2026), stated superintelligence sits on a five-year horizon — roughly 2031 — branding it "personal superintelligence." Already surfaced in the AGI report as an exclusion from that milestone; here it is the primary data point.

**Elon Musk (xAI).** In a July 2026 interview with The Economist, stated AI will surpass "the sum total of human intelligence" within roughly five years (~2031) — explicitly the harder, Yampolskiy-style bar (exceeding all of humanity combined, not just the single best human), distinguishing his claim from Wang's and Hassabis's less specific framings.

**Jared Kaplan (Anthropic, co-founder).** In a December 2025 interview, framed 2027–2030 as the window in which an "intelligence explosion" becomes possible if AI is permitted to train itself — not a superintelligence-arrival date itself, but a dated window for the recursive-self-improvement precursor event that Nexus's own Superintelligence Forecast Report methodology already treats as the key mechanism (see that file's "transition-gap model").

**Aidan Gomez (Cohere).** Said superintelligence "arising from recursive self-improvement... is happening faster than we thought" — an acceleration claim, not a dated prediction.

**Arthur Mensch (Mistral AI).** Rejects the framing outright: existential risk is "ill-defined," and "the whole AGI rhetoric is about creating God. I don't believe in God." No date offered by design, not by omission.

**No public, dated superintelligence-specific statement was found** from: Robin Li (Baidu), Liang Wenfeng (DeepSeek), Yang Zhilin (Moonshot AI), or any thought leader outside Anthropic.

### The Singularity (loss of control)

**Elon Musk (xAI).** The single most concrete data point in this entire survey: in the same July 2026 Economist interview, Musk stated humans will likely lose control of AI's trajectory within a decade — **by roughly 2036** — comparing the future intelligence gap to the gap between humans and chimpanzees ("it is hard to imagine the chimpanzees would be in charge"), and separately puts the probability of AI-driven human extinction at 10–20%. This is a genuine, dated, named loss-of-control prediction — the exact category the Singularity Forecast Report exists to track.

**Dario Amodei (Anthropic).** Published "We Must Pace the Frontier" on September 12, 2026 (already fully logged in `Singularity Forecast Report.md`'s own Evidence Log — see that file for the complete account), warning that without a deliberate industry slowdown, AI could within 6–12 months be capable of "leading a swarm of agents that could take over the entire internet." **This is a contained-incident-scale warning, not a full-Singularity prediction**, per the standing contained-incident-vs-the-event distinction — a swarm disrupting the internet is bounded and (in principle) recoverable, distinct from the irreversible, civilizational-scale loss of control the Singularity Forecast Report actually forecasts. No date was given for the full event itself.

**Sam Altman (OpenAI) and Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind).** Both cosigned Amodei's essay within the same week (already logged in the Singularity Forecast Report). Altman separately stated, in a mid-September 2026 interview, "we could lose control of the future to AI" as one of two named top risks (the other being power concentration) — a qualitative warning with no date attached. Hassabis, in a July 2026 essay, proposed a FINRA-style industry oversight body (also already logged), and when asked for a probability-of-doom figure declined to give one, calling the risk "nonzero and probably non-negligible" — again, no date.

**Jared Kaplan (Anthropic).** The same 2027–2030 decision-window statement above names losing control of the technology as the explicit negative branch of the choice he describes — a Singularity-adjacent framing sharing the same window as his Superintelligence-precursor claim above, not an independent data point.

**Aidan Gomez (Cohere).** The same recursive-self-improvement concern above explicitly names the risk that "the very humans who initially built those systems could lose control" — again Singularity-adjacent, not independently dated.

**Arthur Mensch (Mistral AI).** His rejection of existential-risk framing applies here as well — an explicit, if undated, "no" vote against treating loss-of-control as a near-term concern worth planning around.

**No public, dated Singularity-specific statement was found** from: Robin Li, Liang Wenfeng, Yang Zhilin, or any roster thought leader.

**Not part of this roster, noted for context only:** Yoshua Bengio (not a CEO or thought leader at any of the 16 Who's Who companies) made prominent September 2026 statements urging governments to "wake up" to AI existential risk and warning that catastrophic misuse or power concentration may be as likely as accidental loss of control — directionally consistent with this roster's own recent turn, but excluded from the ledger itself since he falls outside the Who's Who taxonomy this survey is built on.

## First Principles Analysis (Euclid)

The AGI report's structural finding — that every aggressive date comes from someone with a commercial stake in the capability story — holds again here, with one twist: on Superintelligence, the *same* CEOs who benefit from "AGI is close" also benefit from "superintelligence is close," since both claims sell the same story to the same investors. But on the Singularity, the incentive structure inverts. A CEO who says "we might lose control of this" is making a claim *against* their own product's marketability — which is exactly why the recent wave of loss-of-control statements from Amodei, Altman, Hassabis, and Musk carries more evidentiary weight than their capability-timeline claims do: it's evidence that goes against the interest of the person providing it, the single strongest kind of testimony by the standard the AGI report's own Popper objection 1 established. That doesn't mean the *specific dates* attached (only Musk offers one) are well-calibrated — but the *direction* (genuine, rising concern among the people closest to the technology) is harder to dismiss as marketing than the capability-timeline claims are.

A second point: the roster's Superintelligence claims cluster suspiciously close to their own AGI claims — Altman's superintelligence window (2027–2031) barely trails his AGI window (end of 2026); Wang's five-year superintelligence horizon and Musk's five-year "exceed all humanity" horizon both land around 2031, near where their own AGI-adjacent claims already sit. If Superintelligence is genuinely a higher, later bar than AGI — which Nexus's own definitional split insists it is — this compression suggests these speakers are not carefully distinguishing the two milestones in their own minds, are using "superintelligence" loosely to mean "very capable AI" rather than the harder Yampolskiy/Soares bar, or both. Read the roster's Superintelligence dates as evidence about how loosely the term is used industry-wide, not as evidence that the gap between AGI and Superintelligence is actually as short as a few years.

## Devil's Advocate (Popper)

Carrying forward the AGI report's six objections (self-interest bias, small/skewed sample, definitional inconsistency, uneven sourcing confidence, roster decay, silence-isn't-evidence) — all still apply here, at least as strongly, plus two new ones specific to this pair of milestones:

1. **Self-interest bias inverts, but only partially, on the Singularity.** As Euclid notes, a lab CEO warning about loss of control is making an against-interest statement, which should raise confidence in the *direction* of that testimony. But it should not raise confidence in specifics: Amodei's essay explicitly proposes a governance and evaluator-access framework Anthropic is positioned to lead: raising the alarm about loss-of-control risk and then proposing your own company's practices as the industry standard is not free of self-interest, even if the underlying concern is genuine.
2. **Sample size collapses further than it did for AGI.** Only 6 of 16 companies produced a usable Superintelligence statement, and only 2 (with Musk as the sole dated data point) produced a genuine Singularity-timing statement. A "range" built from one dated data point (Musk's 2036) is not a range in any statistical sense — it is a single named individual's guess, reported here because it exists and is attributable, not because it is representative.
3. **The Amodei "Powerful AI" ambiguity (flagged in Sherlock's section) is a real inconsistency risk across Nexus's own two reports**, not just a one-off caveat: if a future reader cites Amodei's statement from the AGI report as AGI evidence and from this report as Superintelligence-adjacent evidence without reading the flag in both places, Nexus's own corpus would look internally inconsistent about what Amodei actually said. This needs to stay flagged in both files going forward, not resolved by arbitrarily picking one milestone.
4. **The contained-incident-vs-the-event distinction is doing real work here and must not be softened for narrative convenience.** Amodei's "swarm of agents could take over the entire internet" warning is dramatic and easy to misread as a Singularity prediction; it is explicitly a 6–12-month, recoverable-in-principle scenario, and the Singularity Forecast Report's own Evidence Log already treats it that way. Any presentation of this report — headline, diagram, or summary — that blurs it into "Anthropic's CEO predicts the Singularity within a year" would be a direct violation of the standing fact-sheet's own core rule.
5. **Musk's specific track record on self-predicted timelines is poor across other domains** (Full Self-Driving, Mars colonization dates, prior AI timelines that have already quietly expired), which should discount the confidence placed in his 2036 figure specifically, even though the underlying *direction* of concern (his own stated 10–20% extinction probability) is harder to wave away, since it works against his own stated preference to keep building rather than pause.

## Forecast (Seldon)

**Superintelligence.** The roster's own statements — Altman (~2027–2031), Amodei's ambiguous 2026–2027 claim, Hassabis (vague, low 2030s inferred), Wang (~2031), Musk (~2031), Kaplan's 2027–2030 precursor window — cluster tightly around 2027–2031, years earlier than Nexus's own standing P5 2029 · P50 2045 · P95 2100. This does **not** move the standing range, for the same reasons the AGI survey didn't move that file's range, amplified: every one of these speakers leads a lab with a direct commercial interest in a "superintelligence is close" narrative, the sample size is even smaller and more skewed than the AGI sample (6 of 16 companies), and — per Euclid's second point above — several of these dates likely reflect loose usage of "superintelligence" to mean "very capable AI" rather than a careful application of the Soares/Yampolskiy bar this file's own Methodology insists on. The Superintelligence Forecast Report's own floor rule (its percentiles must never precede the AGI Forecast Report's own percentiles) is the more load-bearing check here than any of this roster's specific dates. **No change to `Superintelligence Forecast Report.md`.** An Evidence Log entry recording "evidence noted, no change" is appended, explicitly noting the Amodei definitional-ambiguity flag for future reference.

**The Singularity.** This is a thinner but more serious body of evidence. Musk's 2036 figure sits inside the standing range (P5 2027 · P50 2042 · P95 2100) — earlier than the P50 but within the P5–P50 band — and his 10–20% extinction-probability estimate, while not directly comparable to a date range, is a rare instance of a lab leader putting a number on the outcome at all. The broader pattern — four major lab CEOs (Amodei, Altman, Hassabis, and Musk separately) all issuing public loss-of-control warnings within roughly one week of each other in September 2026 — is genuine evidence of rising concern among exactly the people whose actions would need to cause or prevent this event, and it corroborates the qualitative case the Singularity Forecast Report's Evidence Log has already been building since September 12. But that episode is already fully priced into the current range via that file's own daily-report tracking, including its own "Sooner" and "Later" tempering reasoning (labs' voluntary evaluator-access commitments cutting the other way). **This report's contribution is narrower: it adds Musk's specific dated figure (2036, previously not logged) and confirms the roster's overall direction is consistent with, not contradictory to, the range already in place. No change to `Singularity Forecast Report.md`'s P5/P50/P95** — but a new Evidence Log entry is warranted specifically to capture Musk's 2036/10–20% figure, since that specific data point was not previously in the file's record.
