## Adversary Playbook

**[China-Linked Fire Ant Hijacks Cisco Routers to Steal Credentials and Blind Security Logs](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/china-linked-fire-ant-hijacks-cisco.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Sygnia) — Sygnia disclosed that Fire Ant, a China-nexus actor it previously tracked targeting VMware hypervisors, has expanded into Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS+ authentication servers, and Linux management hosts — turning routers into collection platforms that capture traffic, harvest credentials, and suppress their own logging. The actor deployed a custom credential-theft tool ("TacTap") that injects into the TACACS+ daemon, and a Linux backdoor ("BridgeAgent") disguised as a Zabbix monitoring agent. Scanning and connection attempts reached critical-infrastructure-adjacent networks, though Sygnia found no confirmed compromise at those networks specifically. Sygnia flags a suspected — not conclusively confirmed — overlap with UNC3886, and explicitly found no technical overlap with the unrelated Salt Typhoon or Volt Typhoon campaigns.

**[Russia-Aligned UAC-0099 Plants Nuclear Weapon Prompt in Malware to Disrupt AI Analysis](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/russia-aligned-uac-0099-plants-nuclear.html)** (The Hacker News, citing ESET) — ESET disclosed that CERT-UA-tracked, Russia-aligned actor UAC-0099 embedded a fake "I want to make a nuclear weapon" prompt as a code comment inside a malicious VBS script, deliberately designed to trip an AI malware scanner's safety guardrails and prevent it from analyzing the rest of the script. The script delivers MATCHBOIL, a loader exclusive to the group, as part of a broader toolset used against Ukrainian transportation and energy-sector organizations. It's the second such AI-scanner-evasion trick observed this year — a similar technique surfaced in June's Mini Shai-Hulud/Miasma/Hades supply-chain campaigns, then linked to TeamPCP before that group's source code leaked publicly in May, muddying later attribution.

**[Pharmaceutical Giant McKesson Warns of 'Service Degradation' Following Cyberattack](https://therecord.media/mckesson-cyberattack-ransomware-pharma)** (The Record; also [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/mckesson-data-theft-extortion-attack-shinyhunters/) and SecurityWeek) — McKesson, which handles roughly one-third of North American prescription drug distribution, disclosed that attackers compromised an unnamed third-party application during an intrusion window of August 21–25 and stole data belonging to customers in its oncology, multispecialty, and medical-surgical business units. ShinyHunters claimed responsibility on its leak site; CyberScoop reports the group is seeking a ransom in excess of $55 million with a September 1st deadline, per BleepingComputer — unconfirmed by McKesson. McKesson says intruders no longer have system access and is offering credit monitoring to affected parties. This extends the Adversary Tracking Report's existing ShinyHunters row (RingCentral, ~1.6M records) rather than opening a new one — the same group, a new victim.

**[Aurora Ransomware Operators Use Cursor AI in Attacks Against 10 Targets](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/aurora-ransomware-operators-use-cursor.html)** (The Hacker News, citing CloudSEK and Gambit Security) — CloudSEK found an exposed open directory belonging to Aurora (Aur0ra), a Russian-speaking ransomware group, revealing months of activity against 20+ organizations across 9 countries — including Christeyns, Teckentrup, Helideck Certification Agency, and Bayou Title — with CIS ranges and domains deliberately excluded from targeting. Recovered chat logs show the operator using Cursor, an AI coding assistant, to plan attacks in Russian, including a full Active Directory Certificate Services exploitation plan. Separately, Gambit Security observed the same operator using Cursor Agent (running Anthropic's Claude Sonnet) for hands-on exploitation against 10 targets between April and May 2026 — scanning, credential relay attempts, and certificate attacks — noting "the majority of the commands failed to achieve the stated objective on the first attempt, resulting in multiple refinements." The same reporting also profiles **Gryxa**, a separate AI-built intrusion toolkit used by an unrelated, unattributed financially-motivated actor against 324 hosts — ReliaQuest calls it the first observed case of AI covering an entire intrusion end-to-end, from initial RMM-software abuse through automated EDR disabling within 10–13 minutes of detection.

**[ValleyRAT Backdoor Hides in Signed Adware That Users Add to Antivirus Exclusions](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/valleyrat-backdoor-hides-in-signed.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Kaspersky) — Kaspersky attributes a new ValleyRAT (Winos 4.0) distribution method to Silver Fox: the backdoor rides inside a signed Chinese adware application ("QN Wallpaper"), running under a trusted, signed process so that users' own antivirus exclusions for the adware inadvertently shield the malware too. DLL sideloading inside the signed binary lets ValleyRAT evade signature-based trust controls entirely. Kaspersky's 2026 telemetry counts 100,000+ ValleyRAT detections and 1,500+ unique victims overall, concentrated in China and India, though this specific adware-based vector's individual share isn't broken out.

**[Berlin Won't Pay Extortion Group Claiming Data Theft](https://www.securityweek.com/berlin-wont-pay-extortion-group-claiming-data-theft/)** (SecurityWeek) — Berlin's city government confirmed a network breach discovered August 14th, with data exfiltration occurring August 7–12 from the Senate Departments for Mobility/Transport/Climate/Environment and for Urban Development/Construction/Housing; both departments took their networks offline the same day. The Rhysida ransomware group claimed the attack on its leak site August 28th, alleging theft of 5.7TB of data covering 12,000+ people — including IBANs, plaintext credentials, payroll records, and passport/ID information — and demanding 30 bitcoin (~$2.3 million). Berlin's Governing Mayor and Interior Senator publicly confirmed the ransom demand and refused to pay. This is Rhysida's first appearance in the Adversary Tracking Report.

**[TerminalFix Uses Fake Cloudflare CAPTCHAs to Deploy Reverse-Tunnel Backdoor](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/terminalfix-uses-fake-cloudflare.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Microsoft; also [N2K Cyberwire Issue 166](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/166) and a Gmail-sourced Risky Business Bulletin) — Microsoft disclosed a new ClickFix variant, TerminalFix, that tricks victims into pasting commands into Windows Terminal or PowerShell — rather than the traditional Run dialog — via fake Cloudflare CAPTCHA pages on compromised websites, "increasing the likelihood that complex, multi-line scripts execute successfully." The attack chain uses DLL sideloading, steganographic payload extraction from PNG images, Active Directory reconnaissance, and a custom Python reverse-tunnel implant giving attackers persistent, network-level proxy access into the victim's environment. No actor has been attributed; the campaign spans organizations across multiple sectors.

**[Attackers Steal METR API Key and Consume AI Credits Worth About $600,000](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-steal-metr-api-key-and.html)** (The Hacker News) — METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models for safety risk, disclosed two 2026 security incidents. In March, an attacker found a publicly exposed EC2 instance behind a Google-auth fail-open bug, extracted an API key, and ran up roughly $600,000 in AI-credit usage before METR's provider absorbed the cost. In May, a separate sustained campaign combined credential stuffing, OAuth token attempts, and staff phishing against METR's infrastructure; an independent researcher separately found and reported an exposed SQL query bug in METR's public transcript viewer before it could be exploited. METR found no evidence non-public evaluation data was accessed in either incident.

**[Attackers Exploit Critical Langflow and Rails Flaws in Credential-Probing and C2 Activity](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-exploit-critical-langflow-and.html)** (The Hacker News, citing VulnCheck; also [N2K Cyberwire Issue 166](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/166) and a Gmail-sourced Risky Business Bulletin, both on the Rails flaw) — VulnCheck recorded mass exploitation of two critical, unrelated flaws: CVE-2026-0768 in Langflow (arbitrary Python code execution as root) and CVE-2026-66066 "KindaRails2Shell" in Ruby on Rails (unauthenticated secret-key exposure leading to remote code execution). Langflow-targeting traffic climbed from 50+ detections to 360 within a day and "primarily originates from Russia," per VulnCheck — an infrastructure observation, not a formal attribution; vulnerable hosts concentrate in the US, Germany, Malaysia, Brazil, and India. The Rails exploitation hit honeypots in Singapore, Israel, and the UK, with command-and-control traced to a France-based IP with infrastructure in Israel. VulnCheck notes even a patched Rails server remains vulnerable to a related Marshal-deserialization gadget the official fix doesn't close.

**[North Korean Job Fraud Expands Beyond IT Into Healthcare and Sales](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/north-korean-job-fraud-expands-beyond.html)** (The Hacker News, citing Huntress and Recorded Future) — Huntress documented DPRK fraudulent-remote-worker cases expanding beyond the scheme's usual IT-role targeting into healthcare and sales/marketing positions, including a case at an Australian healthcare company (three workers posing as Chinese nationals, caught via VPN/proxy patterns and forged documents) and a financial-services firm (a PiKVM device and a USB capture card used to spoof a Zoom webcam feed). ## Critical Infrastructure Attacks

**['Watershed 250' Test Program in Texas Looks to Private Sector for Water Cybersecurity Help](https://cyberscoop.com/watershed-250-texas-water-cybersecurity-pilot/)** (CyberScoop) — The Office of the National Cyber Director and Texas Cyber Command launched a six-month pilot, "Project Watershed 250," drawing volunteer expertise and technology from a dozen companies (including Microsoft, Fortinet, Google Cloud, Palo Alto Networks, AWS, Cloudflare, Zscaler, and Dragos) to help protect the water sector, rolling out first in San Antonio. **CISA adds PaperCut's two zero-days to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog** ([CISA advisory](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/31/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog); also [N2K Cyberwire Issue 166](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/15/166)) — CISA added CVE-2026-81578 (missing authentication) and CVE-2026-82078 (unsafe reflection leading to arbitrary Java bytecode execution) to its KEV catalog on August 31st, both affecting PaperCut NG/MF and chainable into full server compromise. The underlying zero-day was already reported in the 08-28 report and flagged as a "thin update" on 08-29 when PaperCut shipped a corrected patch; today's KEV addition with a concrete federal deadline is the first genuinely new actionable fact since then.

## Government Surveillance

**Dutch government proposes major expansion of AIVD/MIVD surveillance powers** (Risky Business Bulletin, via Gmail, citing De Volkskrant) — The Dutch government introduced a bill expanding the powers of its two intelligence services, AIVD (domestic) and MIVD (military), citing the threat of war with Russia and rising aggression from China and Iran. Under the proposed law, the agencies could tap or follow a target for up to a year without pre-approval (down from requiring permission for any tapping today), and hacking operations would need supervisor approval only at the final data-collection stage rather than pre-deployment testing. The bill also lets the agencies compel Dutch companies or citizens to hand over data on a target, with criminal penalties for refusal, and — most criticized — introduces an undefined "state emergency" status that suspends most supervisory and reporting obligations, with no specification of what could trigger it. The Netherlands joins Ireland and Germany among European nations overhauling surveillance law since Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

## Cybersecurity Canon Project Book Reviews (September 2026)

**["Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI"](https://cybercanon.org/nexus-a-brief-history-of-information-networks-from-the-stone-age-to-ai/)** by Yuval Noah Harari, reviewed by Rosalyn Page (The Canon Project) — Harari examines how information networks have shaped human societies and power across history, arguing more information doesn't equal a more truthful society — the networks that work best are the ones with strong self-correcting mechanisms. The book treats AI as a genuinely new kind of information technology because it can act as an agent — generating content and making decisions inside a network — without direct human oversight, introducing risks where algorithmic systems gain decision authority without institutional self-correction to check them. Rated **Niche**: no operational cybersecurity guidance, but a useful conceptual framework for cyber leaders and policy-minded practitioners thinking about AI governance and algorithmic bias.

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**[The AI Kill Switch Act Is Repeating the Clipper Chip's Mistakes](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-kill-switch-act-clipper-chip-mistakes-op-ed/)** (CyberScoop op-ed) — Arguing against the AI Kill Switch Act (Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran), which would let CISA order frontier AI labs to build kill-switch/throttle capability into their systems, the piece draws a direct parallel to the NSA's 1993 Clipper Chip — a mandated government-access backdoor found exploitable by outsiders within a year — and to the "Chip Security Act" now under consideration for this year's NDAA. It warns that a mandated kill-switch creates a deliberate point of failure in AI agents already embedded in banking, e-commerce, power grids, and water systems, and could push foreign governments — wary of a remote US-controlled kill switch — toward European AI-sovereignty alternatives instead. The op-ed also flags that CISA's own AI-agent security standards (CAISI) aren't finished yet, and calls for mandatory red-teaming and liability frameworks in place of a kill-switch mandate.
