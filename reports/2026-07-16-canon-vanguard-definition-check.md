# Canon "Vanguard" Definition — What Rick Got Wrong

## Bottom Line

Your restatement swapped the axis. The original "Vanguard" (from the 2026-07-15 Canon reach-strategy report) is defined by **career-identity** (lifelong readers who've made reading their career strategy) and **network-role** (Rogers' "opinion leaders" — the rest of the community follows their lead; that cascade is why "Vanguard" was chosen as the name in the first place). Your restatement describes a third, different trait entirely: **individual expertise-profile** — how self-taught and cross-disciplinary someone is. That's a plausible thing to admire in a person, and it likely correlates with being an opinion leader in real life, but it is not what the term was built to capture. This is a concept conflation, not a memory slip or a deliberate redesign — worth naming as a distinct case in its own right below.

## Side by Side

| | Original ("the Vanguard") | Your restatement |
|---|---|---|
| **Defining axis** | Career-identity + network-role ("the rest of the cyber community takes its cues from them") | Individual expertise-profile (self-taught status, breadth of mastery) |
| **Why "Vanguard"** | A vanguard is the force that goes first, main body following — cascade built into the word | Not addressed; term is borrowed but its cascade meaning isn't in play |
| **"Elite"** | Deliberately excluded in the original's own Popper pass — status meant to come through behavior, not a label | Word avoided, but see "smuggled back in" below |
| **Categories cited** | None — axis is behavioral, not a list of domains | Seven: cyber, business, first principles, national policy, government, commercial, academic |

## The Discrepancies, Itemized

1. **Axis swap (the core error).** The original Vanguard is about *relational position* — how this person's adoption cascades to others. Your restatement is about *cognitive profile* — how much this person knows, across how many fields. These are logically independent traits that happen to correlate (Rogers' opinion leaders tend to be more "cosmopolite"/well-read, which is adjacent to but not the same as autodidact/polymath) — which is likely why the swap felt natural even though it isn't what the definition said.

2. **Category list — not "entirely invented," but not sourced either.** Of your seven categories, three (**government, academic**, and **commercial** as a loose stand-in for industry) are a recognizable — if loose — paraphrase of the Cybersecurity Canon Project's own mission statement, which describes its audience as practitioners from **"industry, government or academia"** (three categories, not seven). The other four — **cyber, business, first principles, national policy** — don't trace to either the original Vanguard report or the Canon's mission statement.

3. **Scope note.** This memo only answers the *descriptive* question — does your restatement match the source? It does not answer the separate *normative* question of whether autodidact/polymath would be a good trait to formally fold into the Vanguard concept going forward. Those are different questions; see Seldon's first forecast below for a take on the second one.

## Where "First Principles" Came From

Corrected from an earlier, less-supported theory that this was Nexus's own internal branding. More plausible source: Cybersecurity Canon founder Rick Howard's 2023 book, **"Cybersecurity First Principles: A Reboot of Strategy and Tactics"** — itself reviewed on cybercanon.org — bleeding into your restatement because you were writing about the Canon in a context where that book and its author are already salient.

## One More Thing, Worth a Line

Your restatement avoids the literal word "elite," matching the original's own stylistic choice. But "polymaths… masters across multiple disciplines" arguably smuggles the same status-claiming framing back in through a different door — it's a competence-superiority claim even without the word "elite" attached to it. Not the core error, but worth flagging since the original went out of its way to avoid this exact framing.

## Seldon — Forward Look (tight)

1. **If autodidact/polymath were formally added as a third axis:** likely net dilution, not precision gain (~70% confidence). It's a harder-to-operationalize competence claim than the original's behavior-based criteria, and risks reintroducing the "elite"/status framing the original deliberately avoided. Carve-out: it could sharpen targeting *if* operationalized as a measurable behavioral proxy rather than a self-identity label — but that's a deliberate redesign, not what's implied by simply folding it in as stated.
2. **"First Principles" naming collision** (Canon founder's book vs. Nexus's own "First Principles Daily Intelligence Report" / Euclid's guiding question): low urgency, moderate latent risk (~55% confidence). Fine internally; worth a one-line disambiguation note if Vanguard messaging ever goes external.
3. **Recurrence risk:** this kind of drift — paraphrasing a multi-clause bundled concept from memory instead of checking the source — is likely to recur at a similar rate for other Nexus-generated concepts with 2+ bundled traits, absent a quick source-check habit at the point of reuse (~70% confidence). Cheap mitigation: a fast source-check before propagating a restated concept into new material.
