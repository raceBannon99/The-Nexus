## Adversary Playbook

**[US Sanctions Iranian Cyber Actors as UK Discloses Power Plant Attack](https://therecord.media/iran-cyberattacks-us-uk)** (The Record, published August 24th) — The US Treasury sanctioned at least six men — Keyvan Fayyaz Ghareh Blagh, Saber Shahbazi Balujeh, Mohammad Reza Kadkhoda'i, Mojtaba Ghal'eh-Kuhi, and two others previously sanctioned — for a hacking operation housed within Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS) that Treasury says has run since 2023, hitting US energy, defense, healthcare, IT, and financial-sector organizations, plus, in summer 2024, local, state, and federal government offices. Four of the men were separately indicted last week for breaching email accounts tied to the Department of Labor, FERC, and UN-affiliated organizations. Treasury says members are "heavily motivated by personal enrichment," with some prioritizing profit over MOIS tasking, and several allegedly stole cryptocurrency from Iranian nationals as well. The sanctions land days after reports that Iranian hackers shut down a small UK power plant for four days — no lost power, no grid-wide effect — the latest in a run of Iranian critical-infrastructure activity since the US began airstrikes on Iran in February, including attacks on water systems in at least 12 US states, a prominent medical-device company, and the FBI director's personal email account.

**[Slovakia Discovers Russian Backdoors in 279 New Traffic Cameras](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cyber-security/slovakia-discovers-russian-backdoors-in-279-new-traffic-cameras-national-security-service-deactivates-offending-units)** (Tom's Hardware; also noted in [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 161](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing)) — Slovakia's National Security Authority found that 279 newly installed NERO R-ONE speed cameras — bought through a Cyprus-based shell company as part of a €30 million EU-funded modernization program — are rebranded CORDON PRO.M cameras from Russian manufacturer Semicon, hardcoded with a list of 12 Russian phone numbers (from St. Petersburg and Kemerovo) that can open a backdoor shell connection via SMS. The cameras also ship with SecureBoot disabled and expose live video feeds to anyone who knows the broadcasting IP, no password required. Slovakia's Interior Ministry has deactivated the affected units and ordered an independent audit; Risky Business notes similar camera models may be in use in Croatia and other Eastern European countries. No confirmed activation of the backdoor has been reported, and current reporting attributes the scheme to the Russian manufacturer rather than to a specific intelligence service.

**[Hackers Infecting Android Car Systems to Build Proxy Botnet](https://therecord.media/android-botnet-china-hackers)** (The Record, published August 24th; also noted in [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 161](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing)) — Kaspersky documented the first known malware campaign built specifically to infect Android-based car head units — the touchscreen computers controlling navigation, music, and Bluetooth — attributing it with high confidence to MoYu Group, an actor tied to the long-running BadBox botnet operation. Attackers abused TWCore, a legitimate update-and-analytics app on head units made by Chinese vendor DoFun, to silently push a hidden downloader called JarService with no user interaction required; DoFun has since patched the distribution flaw. Beyond ad fraud, the malware's main purpose appears to be turning infected head units into reverse proxies that route other people's internet traffic through a car's connection. Kaspersky notes that despite law-enforcement disruption of the original BadBox botnet in December 2024, individual actors linked to it continue infecting devices worldwide.

**[Operation QUICSILVER Targets Myanmar Government and IT with QUICAgent Backdoor](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/operation-quicsilver-targets-myanmar.html)** (The Hacker News, published August 24th) — A China-nexus actor, assessed with moderate confidence, has run an espionage campaign against Myanmar's government and IT sectors since at least April 2026, using graduation-ceremony-invitation lures in Burmese and a fake Belgian-Myanmar holiday calendar to deliver a Golang-based backdoor called QUICAgent. The multi-stage infection chain hides a Windows shortcut that silently launches Microsoft's legitimate ftp.exe to reconstruct a payload from fragments hidden inside a decoy document; QUICAgent then communicates over QUIC via UDP port 443, using random-delay and hashing-based sandbox evasion, and supports file operations, directory browsing, and remote command execution.

**[Exposing AnonyMousKIT: AI-Powered PhaaS Supply Chain](https://socradar.io/blog/anonymouskit-ai-phaas-supply-chain/)** (SOCRadar, published August 24th, via a Gmail Google Alert digest) — SOCRadar's Threat Research Unit unmasked AnonyMousKIT, a credit-metered Phishing-as-a-Service platform built specifically to defeat Apple's Activation Lock on stolen iPhones, iPads, and Macs. The platform automates credential harvesting across email, SMS, and WhatsApp, and — its key innovation — AI voice agents that impersonate "Alice from Apple Support" for roughly ten cents a call, extracting device passcodes, Apple IDs, and live two-factor codes from victims in over 30 countries, with the heaviest volumes in South Africa, Indonesia, Italy, and India, and a Portuguese-language voice-calling operation concentrated in Brazil. A coding flaw exposed the platform's own operational logs, revealing it is one of 30 backend installations sharing a common codebase across a 506-domain, 168-storefront reseller ecosystem. SOCRadar identified the developer only by the email handle underc0deapple@gmail.com and traced the seller to a Telegram identity calling itself "Owner of AnonyMousTeam"; the platform was still actively operating as of SOCRadar's last collection date.

**[WordlistLoader Delivers Amatera via ClickFix, SynkLoader Phishes Windows Passwords](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/wordlistloader-delivers-amatera-via.html)** (The Hacker News, published August 24th) — Researchers detailed two unrelated, fully unattributed loader campaigns. WordlistLoader spreads through ClearFake/ClickFix-style fake-CAPTCHA prompts on compromised websites, using the EtherHiding technique — fetching malicious code from blockchain smart contracts to resist takedown — to ultimately deliver the Amatera information stealer. SynkLoader impersonates Microsoft Teams IT Service Desk messages to trick corporate employees into running an MSI installer hosted on Azure blob storage, deploying seven modules including a credential-stealing fake Windows lock screen ("PhishLocker"), persistence mechanisms, remote-access-trojan functionality, and VNC streaming; researchers suspect it supports a ransomware group or initial-access broker, though no specific operator has been named.

## Law Enforcement Disruption

**[Indian Man Who Fled US Arrested on Charges He Helped Scammers Siphon $7.5 Million From the Elderly](https://therecord.media/cyber-scam-indian-arrested)** (The Record, published August 24th) — The Justice Department charged Jay Sunilbharthi Goswami, a 21-year-old Indian national on a student visa, with acting as a money mule for India-based scammers who convinced at least nine elderly victims in New York and New Jersey — most in their 70s and 80s — to hand over more than $7.5 million in cash and gold after being falsely told their identities were linked to crimes or financial fraud. Goswami allegedly collected the funds in person and relayed them to India, earning roughly $90,000. After his August 17th arrest and release pending trial, he fled to Canada via the Peace Bridge and was detained by Canadian authorities at a Toronto airport gate en route to Doha; he now faces extradition back to the US and up to 20 years in prison on wire fraud and money-laundering charges.

**[Venezuelan Gets Record Federal Prison Term for ATM Jackpotting](https://www.securityweek.com/venezuelan-gets-record-federal-prison-term-for-atm-jackpotting/)** (SecurityWeek; also noted in [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 161](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing)) — Juan Manuel Gouveia-Aguilera, 27, was sentenced in federal court in Omaha, Nebraska to 96 months in prison — described by prosecutors as the longest federal sentence ever imposed for an individual's role in ATM jackpotting — for a conspiracy that deployed malware to steal more than $3.5 million from ATMs across the US. The court also imposed a five-year term of supervised release. Prosecutors say jackpotting schemes like this one help fund violent transnational criminal organizations, including Tren de Aragua.

## Zero Trust Tactics

**[Attackers Target miniOrange SAML Flaws That Can Grant WordPress Admin Access](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/attackers-target-miniorange-saml-flaws.html)** (The Hacker News, published August 25th) — Attackers are actively scanning WordPress sites running the Xecurify miniOrange SAML 2.0 Single Sign-On plugin for two unauthenticated flaws: CVE-2026-61979 (CVSS 8.1), a privilege-escalation bug from signature-algorithm confusion, and CVE-2026-15981 (CVSS 9.8), an authentication bypass that treats a PHP openssl_verify() error return as a valid signature. Both let an attacker forge a malformed SAML response to sign in as any user, including administrators. Patchstack has observed scanning activity from six IP addresses, described as opportunistic rather than a targeted campaign; proof-of-concept code is public, and versions 17.0.5 (Standard edition) and 17.0.6 fix the issues.

**[Actively Exploited Oracle WebLogic Flaw Lets Unauthenticated Attackers Access Critical Data](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/actively-exploited-oracle-weblogic-flaw.html)** (The Hacker News, published August 25th) — CISA added CVE-2026-21962, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) improper-access-control flaw in Oracle HTTP Server and the WebLogic Server Proxy Plug-in, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 24th, giving federal agencies until August 27th to patch. The flaw lets an unauthenticated, network-based attacker create, delete, or modify critical data on affected servers. Oracle patched it in January 2026, but GreyNoise and CloudSEK have since documented ongoing exploitation attempts — a distinct, more specific vulnerability than the broad set of WebLogic patches noted in Oracle's August Critical Patch Update covered in the 08-20 report.

## Cybersecurity Canon Project Book Reviews (August 2026)

**["Hack to the Future: How World Governments Relentlessly Pursue and Domesticate Hackers"](https://cybercanon.org/hack-to-the-future-how-world-governments-relentlessly-pursue-and-domesticate-hackers/)**, by Emily Crose — Reviewed by Jose Miguel Parrella, published August 24th. Parrella calls it a Hall of Fame-worthy recommendation. Crose traces hacker culture's evolution across four phases — from early exploratory hacking, through 1980s–90s mainstream visibility, to the late-1990s/2000s dual rise of professionalization and hacktivism, and finally the last 15 years of institutional integration — drawing on interviews, archival material, and FOIA-derived records to connect that history directly to today's penetration-testing, vulnerability-research, and responsible-disclosure norms. The book broadens beyond the US to Europe, Australia, and post-Soviet states, documents women's under-told role in early hacker communities, and traces hacktivism's intersection with the Arab Spring and Euromaidan protests. Parrella's takeaway: understanding cybersecurity's cultural and historical origins is foundational context for practitioners, not optional color.

## Compliance Trends

**[Uber Fined Nearly $1 Billion by Dutch Regulators Over Automated Suspensions of Driver Accounts](https://www.securityweek.com/uber-fined-nearly-1-billion-by-dutch-regulators-over-automated-suspensions-of-driver-accounts/)** (SecurityWeek; also noted in [N2K Cyberwire's Daily Briefing, Issue 161](https://thecyberwire.com/newsletters/daily-briefing)) — The Dutch Data Protection Authority fined Uber €825 million (about $966 million) — the second-largest GDPR penalty ever issued, behind only Ireland's 2023 €1.2 billion fine against Meta — for using fully automated systems to suspend, and in some cases permanently deactivate, driver accounts between 2018 and 2022 without genuine human review or adequate notice to affected drivers. GDPR bars fully automated decisions with significant consequences for individuals unless a human is meaningfully involved and the person has a way to contest the outcome. The case originated from a French complaint and was handled by the Dutch regulator because Uber's European headquarters sit in the Netherlands; Uber says it "strongly disagrees" and plans to appeal, arguing its current suspension process already includes human review and a dispute path.
