# Kim Zetter on *Countdown to Zero Day*: Every Findable Appearance in Print, Audio, and Video, 2014–2026

**Question:** As the Cybersecurity Canon Project CEO, I'm interviewing Kim Zetter (Canon Hall of Fame Author) tomorrow (2026-11-10) as part of our partnership with the Cyber Guild ([registration](https://thecyberguild.org/events/community-book-club-countdown-to-zero-day/)) about her book *Countdown to Zero Day*. She published it more than a decade ago. Provide the citations for every place Kim talked about the book in print or on YouTube since she published.

**Date:** 2026-11-09 · **Format:** Annotated bibliography with interview-prep notes

---

## Bottom Line (Bradlee)

Kim Zetter has talked about *Countdown to Zero Day* in public at least 43 times since Crown published it on November 11, 2014, and the record is public. The appearances run from a Marketplace radio interview the day before publication to a House Homeland Security hearing on Stuxnet's 15th anniversary in July 2025. Since then she has published new Stuxnet-era reporting as recently as May 2026. All of them are cited in the Sources section below. Thirty-two bear directly on the book or on Stuxnet, including four pieces of her own later reporting that extend it. Six are adjacent panels where the book is her calling card but the topic drifted elsewhere, and five have a topic or date that couldn't be confirmed. Those are labeled so you don't build a question on the wrong one.

The appearances come in three waves. The **launch tour (Nov. 2014–2016)** was book-centered: Marketplace, KQED Forum, Risky Business #351, Dale Peterson's S4x15 podcast, a Stanford CISAC seminar, Radio New Zealand, a Sonoma State colloquium, New America's podcast, and conference panels in Tallinn/Washington, Stockholm, Oslo and Tel Aviv. The **reflective middle (2017–2021)** treated Stuxnet as a reference point for newer events. Examples include the Limn interview comparing it to the DNC hack, keynotes in Kyiv and Kraków, Darknet Diaries #29, the OODAcast, two RSAC 2021 sessions, Synack's *WE'RE IN!*, and her 2021 Zero Day newsletter coverage of the Natanz sabotage. The **"Stuxnet as history" phase (2022–2026)** includes her Black Hat USA 2022 keynote "Pre-Stuxnet, Post-Stuxnet: Everything Has Changed, Nothing Has Changed," BlueHat IL 2023, the Cybersecurity Defenders and Whale Hunting podcasts, the July 22, 2025 congressional testimony, NPR's *Throughline* (March 2026) and CyCon 2026.

For tomorrow, the most useful material is where she has gone beyond the book:

- **The Dutch mole (Yahoo News, Sept. 2019).** With Huib Modderkolk, she reported that an Iranian engineer recruited by Dutch intelligence on behalf of the CIA and Mossad gave Stuxnet's first version its way into Natanz. That fills a gap the 2014 book could only describe as an open question about how the code crossed the air gap.
- **Fast16 (Zero Day, May 2026).** She reported expert confirmation that the Fast16 malware sabotaged nuclear-weapons tests, likely in Iran, and argued it predates Stuxnet. That reopens the book's central claim that Stuxnet was "the world's first digital weapon."
- **Her own verdict on Stuxnet's effect.** At Stanford in 2015 she said Stuxnet had "very little effect" on Iran's program and that the U.S. lost moral standing on digital weapons. Ask whether she still holds that view after a decade of Iranian conflict, including the Iran war era *Throughline* covered.
- **"Everything has changed, nothing has changed."** Her 2022 Black Hat framing is a good structure for a "10+ years later" book-club conversation: what the book got right, what it couldn't know, and what readers should take from it now.

The Canon's own connection is your January 2015 Canon review, which inducted the book into what is now the Hall of Fame. It is cited below.

**Caveat:** "every" means every appearance that can be found with public search. Live talks that were never recorded, paywalled print, and podcasts with poor show notes almost certainly exist beyond this list. This is a floor, not a census.

## Adversary Playbook (Ryan)

This is not an adversary-tracking engagement. The question is about an author's public record, so no Kill Chain, Diamond Model, or ATT&CK characterization is warranted. One attribution-hygiene note for the interview, because the vocabulary gets blurred:

- **Operation Olympic Games** is the reported *operation/campaign* name.

- **Equation Group** is a *vendor-assigned* (Kaspersky, 2015) name for an actor linked to Stuxnet-related tooling.

## First-Principles Read (Euclid)

What must be true for a citation list like this to be useful in a live interview?

1. **It must separate what she *said about the book* from what she *reported after it*.** An author talking up a book in 2014 and a journalist adding new facts in 2019 and 2026 are different kinds of evidence. The second kind is where the best interview questions come from, because it shows what the book couldn't know. The catalog therefore flags *extends the book* items.
2. **It must be dated by event, not by upload.** Several videos were uploaded months after the talk (e.g., the Black Hat 2022 keynote was given in August and uploaded in November). Mixing the two would make her views look later than they were.
3. **"Every" can't be proven, only bounded.** Completeness for a public figure's spoken record is not verifiable from search. The honest product is a dated, checked floor with its search method stated, which is what this is.

The first-principles answer: she has been unusually consistent over twelve years. Stuxnet was a threshold event, the U.S. normalized digital sabotage by using it first, and the policy questions it raised are still open. What has *moved* is the factual record around the book, not her thesis. The interview should go where the facts moved.

## Pushback (Popper)

1. **YouTube search undercounts.** YouTube ranks results by relevance and caps them, and talks titled with only an event name (e.g., "Third Session: Building Cyber…") surface only by luck. *Likely missing: unindexed conference keynotes.*
2. **Some "appearances" are thin.** The NPR *Throughline* episode (2026) names her as "writer for WIRED," which suggests archival tape rather than a new interview. The CyCon 2026 conversation and the Cyberlaw Podcast #356 descriptions don't confirm Stuxnet as a topic. Counting these as "talked about the book" overstates the record.
3. **"Print" is the weakest category.** The WIRED excerpt URL and the Yahoo News Dutch-mole original couldn't be opened directly. Citing mirrors is honest but second-best.
4. **The C-SPAN *Communicators* episode** turned up in search with no date. It could be pre- or post-publication.

## New Skills (Turing)

**None built.** One candidate technique is noted for Rick's judgment. When building an author or speaker appearance bibliography, harvest YouTube search results same-origin from a Chrome tab: parse `ytInitialData` across multiple queries, then fetch each watch page for `uploadDate` and description keywords. Sanitize the output to plain characters, because the Chrome tool blocks results that look like query strings. This beat web search by a wide margin. Web search surfaced about 4 videos, while direct harvesting surfaced 25 candidates, 21 of them confirmed Zetter appearances. If Canon author interviews become a recurring prep task, this is worth packaging as a `nexus-author-appearance-bibliography` skill.
