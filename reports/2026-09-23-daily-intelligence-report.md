## Adversary Playbook

**[ShinyHunters claims attack on FBI, demands report retraction](https://cyberscoop.com/shinyhunters-claims-fbi-attack/)** — The prolific cybercrime group ShinyHunters claims to have breached the FBI itself, temporarily defacing FBIjobs.gov Monday and asserting theft of "very sensitive data on almost all FBI agents and individuals who filed an application with the FBI for a job." The group says the attack is retaliation for a May 2026 FBI public service announcement (issued after ShinyHunters' Instructure/Canvas breach) that it says contains inaccurate claims about its tactics; rather than demanding a ransom, ShinyHunters gave the agency one week to correct or remove the advisory. The FBI confirmed it is investigating; the Special Agent Application Portal and apply.fbijobs.gov remained unavailable as of this morning. This is the same group that hijacked rival ransomware gang Cl0p's own leak site last weekend (see [[Adversary Tracking Report]] → Cybercrime → row 20) — a rare instance of a tracked cybercrime group directly targeting a federal law-enforcement agency rather than a commercial victim. [SecurityWeek's own coverage](https://www.securityweek.com/) corroborates directly.

**[Microsoft and partners disrupt EvilTokens, an AI-integrated cybercrime platform](https://cyberscoop.com/microsoft-eviltokens-cybercrime-service-takedown/)** — Microsoft, together with Health-ISAC, Cloudflare, OpenAI, Shadowserver, TRM Labs, Coinbase, and SpyCloud, disrupted "EvilTokens," a phishing-as-a-service platform launched in February 2026 whose chatbot component analyzed victims' inboxes to identify trusted relationships and payment authorizations for business-email-compromise fraud. "AI was not simply helping attackers write more convincing messages," Microsoft's Steven Masada wrote. "It helped them decide who to target, who to impersonate, and how to most effectively exploit the relationship." The service is attributed to Storm-2992; UK Metropolitan Police arrested two named individuals, Felix Utomi and Waidi Segun Adams, on September 18. Investigators linked EvilTokens to more than 12,000 compromised Microsoft customer inboxes across 10,000+ organizations in 79 countries, with at least $1.7 million in confirmed FBI IC3-linked losses — a figure Microsoft calls conservative. Acting on a September 15 federal court order, Microsoft seized 50 websites and disabled 175+ supporting domains. See [[Adversary Tracking Report]] → Cybercrime → row 69. *Also relevant under AI Singularity Timeline below.*

**[Chinese threat actor UTA0565 confirmed as fourth group sharing the BlueMoon exploit kit](https://cyberscoop.com/volexity-uta0565-china-exploit-chain-chrome-microsoft/)** — Volexity researchers disclosed that a China-aligned group it tracks as UTA0565 exploited a Chrome/Windows zero-day chain (CVE-2026-85046, CVE-2026-87491, CVE-2026-85880) between September 3-4, deploying a previously undocumented malware family it calls CLEANGULP. UTA0565 phished Asian government entities with fake websites impersonating the Center for American Progress and China Digital Times, urging targets to publicly support imprisoned Hong Kong activist Chow Hang-tung. Critically, UTA0565 is the fourth distinct Chinese cluster confirmed using the "BlueMoon" exploit kit already tracked in this report's Adversary Tracking Report (alongside TA412/APT31, UNK_LateNight, and UNK_DoubleCheck/UNK_QuietRacket) — Volexity assesses the kit's spread as "a coordinated effort within the Chinese computer network exploitation community, where the core kit was likely shared, customized, and weaponized by multiple groups." See [[Adversary Tracking Report]] → China → rows 20 and 28. Corroborated independently via [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/chinese-hackers-exploit-chrome-windows.html).

**[UK NCSC: defenders "simply cannot put AI to work" the way attackers can](https://therecord.media/ai-set-to-help-attackers-more-than-defenders)** — Dave Chismon, chief technology officer for architecture at Britain's National Cyber Security Centre, published an official assessment arguing that AI structurally favors attackers over defenders and will likely widen that gap. Offensive AI use has an unambiguous success signal — an exploit works or it doesn't — that automated tooling handles well; defensive AI actions, by contrast, are taken on live production systems with no comparable signal and a steep asymmetric downside (a bad automated patch can cause the same disruption an attacker was trying to prevent), so a human must still weigh and answer for every defensive move. Chismon's comments come as the NCSC builds its own "Cyber Shield" agentic-defense capability and prepares a formal "AI for Cyber Defence" research agenda; he cautions that autonomous defense "will take time, effort, and research" and organizations "cannot risk just waiting for agentic defence to roll in and protect them." Seldon's assessment: a credentialed national cyber-defense official publicly stating the attacker/defender AI-capability gap is structural rather than closing corroborates rather than newly informs the diffusion trajectory already priced into the September 14 P5 shift, since it's an assessment of an existing gap rather than a new capability threshold. Full reasoning in [[Singularity Forecast Report]]'s Evidence Log.

**[Rep. Gottheimer introduces AI Cyber Defense Act for water-sector protection](https://cyberscoop.com/gottheimer-ai-cyber-defense-act-cisa-pilot/)** — Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ), a co-chair of the House Democratic Commission on Artificial Intelligence, introduced legislation directing CISA to establish a $100 million (2027-2031) pilot program giving critical-infrastructure operators free access to frontier AI models for cybersecurity defense. The bill, backed by a bipartisan slate of co-sponsors, follows a summer of cyberattacks on water facilities and is separate from — but adjacent to — the Office of the National Cyber Director's existing Texas-based "Project Watershed 250" pilot, which relies on purely voluntary private-sector participation with no dedicated budget.
