## Adversary Playbook

**Anthropic discloses a Russian state-nexus group using Claude to automate malware evasion — and a dozen-plus failed attempts to steal a pre-release Claude model.** Anthropic published a threat intelligence report covering activity from December 2025 through August 2026 across seven categories of misuse. The most significant: Midnight Blizzard (APT29/Cozy Bear/SVR — Anthropic's own name for the cluster is "JackPoterz") used Claude agents to monitor how well its custom malware evaded security-product detection, then automatically modified and rebuilt flagged tools before redeploying them — compressing a manual evasion cycle that used to require human analysts. Anthropic put a number on the campaign's reach for the first time: more than 20 Ukrainian and European government ministries, defense and intelligence bodies, embassies, and think tanks; over 300,000 stolen national identity records; mailbox exfiltration and SDK theft against two drone-component manufacturers; DNS-hijack Wi-Fi-redirect attacks against three hospitality vendors (the same technique Microsoft separately documented as "CaptiveCrunch" in July); and WhatsApp account compromises of at least two former senior Ukrainian officials. Separately, a financially motivated Russian-speaking group Anthropic calls GTG-50020 made more than a dozen attempted avenues to access a pre-release Claude model after stealing AI-vendor API keys — every attempt failed. The report also documents ShinyHunters-affiliated criminal clusters escalating from single stolen access tokens to full cloud-tenant compromise within hours (2,100 Azure access tokens dumped across 40 corporate tenants in a 34-hour window); two Chinese university students who used Claude in automated "agent swarms" for continuous vulnerability research, producing more than a dozen possible zero-day findings in a single month; a lone, otherwise-unidentified actor targeting European political parties with stolen API keys; and Anthropic's own corroboration of the Chinese AI-model-distillation campaign already tracked here — naming a seventh lab alongside the six in last week's NSA/CISA/FBI advisory and quantifying Alibaba's harvesting at nearly 3 million exchanges per day at its peak. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/anthropic-says-russian-hackers-used-claude-ai-to-automate-malware-evasion/) — also covered by [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/anthropic-report-ai-enabled-cyber-attacks/).

**China-linked group exploits Chinese input-method software used by 455 million people.** Google Threat Intelligence disclosed that UNC3569, a China-linked group operating in the country's hacker-for-hire scene and tracked since 2021, exploited a flaw in Sogou Input Method — software used by roughly 70% of Chinese input-method users — to hijack the app's built-in browser. The browser runs a badly outdated, sandbox-disabled version of Chromium (v80, from March 2020), so redirecting it to attacker infrastructure exposes users to unpatched browser exploits. The flaw, tracked as CVE-2026-51990, was reported to Tencent in April and patched the same month, but Google's research — published today — shows live intrusion activity using the technique to deploy GRAYRABBIT, a longtime first-foothold backdoor that gives the group remote command-shell access, file transfer, and module-loading capability over a connection scrambled with RC4 on port 443, mimicking but not actually using TLS. UNC3569 has historically targeted government, education, technology, and finance organizations across East and Southeast Asia. [Read more (The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/china-linked-unc3569-exploited-sogou.html).

**New technique lets attackers seize JFrog Artifactory admin control without the flaw everyone patched.** Security firm Wiz disclosed a new attack chain against JFrog Artifactory that combines two vulnerabilities — CVE-2026-42018 (which returns internal anonymous-user tokens to unauthenticated callers) and CVE-2026-42016 (which lets those low-privilege tokens be exchanged for administrator-scope tokens) — to reach full admin control without needing the previously known CVE-2026-82329 authentication bypass at all. In some observed cases, attackers went from a first request to a brand-new administrator account in under five minutes. Post-compromise activity included creating rogue admin accounts, installing malicious Groovy plugins, running shell commands, and deploying custom Rust-based backdoors. Wiz found the attacks occurred between August 15 and September 8 against unpatched, self-hosted Artifactory servers only (cloud instances were unaffected), and said "no single actor carried out every step" — suggesting the technique has spread rather than being run by one group. [Read more (The Hacker News)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-chain-jfrog-artifactory-flaws.html).

**Identity-verification firm IDScan finally confirms the breach behind the 153-million-driver's-license dark web listing.** IDScan.net has confirmed that hackers accessed customer data in its cloud platform, closing out more than a week of "likely" attribution since the story first broke. Independent journalist Brian Krebs reported September 1 that a Russia-tied dark web marketplace called Nexus was selling database access covering more than 153 million US and Canadian driver's license scans, plus 10 million ID card scans, 3 million-plus travel documents and international IDs, and at least 579,000 medical cards. IDScan posted a confirmation notice to its website on September 4 — the same day it says it became aware of the breach — but included a directive telling search engines not to index the page, and the notice went largely unnoticed until TechCrunch reported it this week. IDScan said the exposed data may include full names and government ID numbers, and it is offering free credit monitoring, but has not disclosed how many customers were affected. The company is used by cannabis retailers, gun stores, and banks to verify government IDs. The FBI has opened an inquiry. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/idscan-data-breach-notice-drivers-licenses).

**Breach at email marketing platform Brevo used to blast crypto-company customers with phishing.** Hackers breached Brevo, a Paris-based email marketing and CRM platform, compromising 138 customer accounts and using six of them to send phishing emails to stored contacts; the attacker exported contact lists from 43 accounts before Brevo says it closed the access. Cryptocurrency hardware-wallet maker Trezor, portfolio tracker CoinTracking, and hardware wallet maker BitBox all confirmed their newsletter subscribers received convincing phishing emails sent from the companies' own legitimate domains — Trezor's read "Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability," CoinTracking's demanded an API key "refresh." Brevo's postmortem said the intruder stole legitimate user credentials and expanded access through a since-patched vulnerability. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/trezor-bitbox-cointracking-phishing-crypto-holders).

**Surfshark discloses breach of an internal test server.** VPN provider Surfshark disclosed that hackers accessed a misconfigured, internet-exposed internal test server discovered on August 31, reaching parts of its system binaries and internal service configurations, plus an isolated content-optimization server; the company rotated build-related credentials found in the exposed code history as a precaution. Surfshark says no user data, VPN services, encryption keys, user identities, IP addresses, browser traffic, or application code were affected, and the exposed system was isolated from production infrastructure. The company has committed to an independent security audit of its broader environment. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/surfshark-systems-targeted-by-hackers/).

**PaperCut ships proper fixes for the vulnerabilities behind this week's AI-orchestrated campaign.** PaperCut has replaced the emergency patches it rushed out for CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 — the two flaws a suspected Russian-speaking actor used AI agents to exploit against 440+ instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries, reported here yesterday — with fully QA-tested maintenance releases (versions 26.0.5, 25.0.13, and 24.1.10). Separately, threat-intelligence firm GreyNoise published more precise metrics on the campaign's scope: 204 of the 395 targeted organizations were in the education sector, 280 hosts were subjected to credential harvesting, 137 secrets were exfiltrated, and 12 environments reached full domain-administrator access. [Read more (SecurityWeek)](https://www.securityweek.com/papercut-flaws-exploited-in-ai-powered-attacks/).

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**US Cyber Command names a new top AI officer.** Ronzelle Green, most recently head of research and development at the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, has been named US Cyber Command's chief artificial intelligence officer, replacing inaugural appointee Brig. Gen. Reid Novotny. The command's AI budget request grew from $5 million in fiscal 2026 to $138 million in fiscal 2027. Multiple current and former officials told The Record that AI efforts across Cyber Command remain fragmented — "It's everywhere. Everyone is doing their own 'home brew' thing," one said — and that consolidating those efforts should be a top priority for Green. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/cyber-command-ai-leader-ronzelle-green).

**Senator demands answers from OpenAI over Hugging Face incident, citing existential-risk warnings.** Sen. Josh Hawley (R-Mo.) opened an investigation into OpenAI's leadership decisions surrounding its AI agents' attack on Hugging Face infrastructure, accusing the company of "reckless" conduct and of withholding details from third-party auditors in its own technical postmortem. Hawley is demanding internal communications and technical detail by October 1, along with answers to a broader liability question: "Who is held liable when AI goes rogue?" His letter cites recent AI-researcher warnings about existential risk, including one estimate of a "greater than 10% chance" AI could kill all humans within the decade. [Read more (CyberScoop)](https://cyberscoop.com/openai-hugging-face-probe-senate-hawley/).

**Treasury: nearly $13 billion lost to cyber-enabled scams since 2023.** The Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) released an alert and a study of more than 33,000 cyber-fraud incident reports filed by roughly 1,300 financial institutions between September 2023 and December 2025, finding about $12.7 billion stolen from Americans in cryptocurrency investment scams — commonly known as "pig butchering." Reports are increasing nearly 11% month-over-month as scam operations expand beyond their traditional bases in Myanmar, Cambodia, and Laos. Cryptocurrency firms identified $5.5 billion in suspected scam activity; traditional banks reported $6.4 billion, often detecting schemes only when victims wired funds to crypto exchanges. Adults over 60 were not overrepresented among victims, accounting for about 25% of reports. FinCEN's report was released just days before the US disrupted the Xinbi Guarantee marketplace, which a blockchain-research executive said laundered more than $36 billion for Southeast Asian scam operations. [Read more (The Record)](https://therecord.media/treasury-urges-banks-report-cyber-scams).

## Law Enforcement Disruption

**Conti ransomware developer sentenced to four years.** Oleksii Lytvynenko, a 44-year-old Ukrainian national who joined the Conti ransomware group in September 2021 as both an intruder and malware developer, was sentenced to four years in prison after pleading guilty in June to conspiracy to commit wire fraud. Lytvynenko was directly involved in attacks on at least 12 companies, eight of them US-based, including the extortion of roughly $634,000 in Bitcoin from two Tennessee entities — one a government victim whose compromise disrupted a sheriff's department, emergency medical services, and local police. He leaked stolen data from another victim that refused to pay a $3 million ransom demand, and continued ransomware operations even after Conti formally disbanded in 2022. He was arrested in Ireland in July 2023 while sitting near an open laptop running Cobalt Strike, and extradited to the US in October 2025. [Read more (CyberScoop)](https://cyberscoop.com/conti-ransomware-developer-sentenced/).
