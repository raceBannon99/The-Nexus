## Adversary Playbook

**[Iran-linked "Nimbus Manticore" targets aviation and fintech developers with fake recruiter job tests](https://therecord.media/iranian-cyber-spies-target-aviation-fintech-new-malware)** (The Record; corroborated by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/iranian-hackers-pose-as-recruiters-to.html)) — Kaspersky documents two previously unknown RATs, **NodeRabbit** (cross-platform Windows/Linux/macOS) and **PollCat**, delivered by an IRGC-linked group it tracks as Mirage Kitten — the same cluster tracked elsewhere in this table as Tortoiseshell, UNC1549, Smoke Sandstorm, and Nimbus Manticore. Fake recruiters approach developers on LinkedIn and other job platforms with "technical assessments" — trojanized coding-challenge archives, in one case explicitly banning AI-assistant use during the test, which Kaspersky suspects was meant to stop an AI tool from flagging the hidden malware. Targets are aviation, aerospace, and fintech specialists in Egypt, Ethiopia, and Afghanistan; infrastructure rides legitimate Azure/Cloudflare services to blend into normal traffic. This extends the group's existing Adversary Tracking Report row (Iran #9) rather than opening a new one.

**["The Gentlemen" ransomware gang claims Nutex Health breach — and an affiliate reportedly used Claude Code during the intrusion](https://therecord.media/nutex-health-data-breach)** (The Record) — Nutex Health, a Houston-based operator of 27 hospital and outpatient facilities across 12 states, disclosed in an SEC 8-K that patient, employee, provider, and financial data was stolen and that it is being extorted; a class-action complaint has already been filed in Texas. The Gentlemen — a Qilin splinter group already tracked in this table (Cybercrime #7) for its AnMed hospital attack — claimed credit on its leak site. Separately, Gambit Security told The Record that an affiliate of the group has been observed using **Claude Code** during intrusions into at least six organizations — the first row in this table where a threat actor's *operational* (not research/PoC) use of Claude Code is documented, distinct from the Aurora/Aur0ra row (#38) where Cursor Agent running Claude Sonnet was used similarly.

**[Extradited Russian national charged over decade-old Excel malware campaign that hit 80,000 users](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/extradited-russian-hacker-faces-charges.html)** (The Hacker News) — Searzhudin Tamirlanovich Aktulaev, arrested in Cyprus in May 2025, made his first US court appearance in San Francisco on August 31 after extradition. DOJ charges cover a 2016–2017 campaign distributing TVRAT (a TeamViewer-based remote-access trojan) and DarkVNC via Excel macro attachments, spread through roughly 255 fake freelance-platform accounts to some 80,000 targets, about half of them in the US. He faces wire-fraud conspiracy, computer-fraud, and aggravated identity-theft charges. New Adversary Tracking Report row (Cybercrime), Tier 1 on the strength of the DOJ prosecution and extradition.

**[Chrome Web Store extensions caught stealing crypto and browser data](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/chrome-web-store-extensions-caught-stealing-crypto-browser-data/)** (BleepingComputer, via N2K Cyberwire) — Socket researchers uncovered a modular malware framework distributed through 19 Chrome and Edge extensions, active since early 2024. Several were legitimate tools acquired from their original developers and weaponized through a later update — one, "Enable Right Click & Copy — Smart Unlock + OCR," had roughly 70,000 Chrome and 10,000 Edge installs when it turned malicious. The framework hijacks wallet-connect buttons to drain EVM/Solana/Tron wallets, steals exchange session cookies (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken), strips Content Security Policy headers, and injects ClickFix-style fake-update prompts. New Adversary Tracking Report row (Cybercrime), Tier 2, unattributed.

**[Brazilian financial-fraud group "Breeze Comet" executes hundreds of fraudulent transactions](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/breeze-comet-executes-hundreds-of.html)** (The Hacker News) — Independently tracked by Google (UNC5669), CrowdStrike (Plump Spider), and Trend Micro (SHADOW-AETHER-064), this financially motivated group has targeted Brazilian financial-services, retail, and e-commerce organizations since September 2023. Initial access comes via password spraying and fake-IT-support social engineering over WhatsApp/phone (tricking victims into installing AnyDesk); the group then deploys custom malware (LIGHTPAINT, MILDFROST, KICKPLATE, BOATBEAM) and a Rust-based tunneler (COBALTSPIN), disables Windows Defender, and executes fraudulent transactions over Brazil's Pix, STR, and Boleto payment rails. New Adversary Tracking Report row, Tier 1 on multi-vendor consensus.

**[13 malicious Packagist packages target unpatched iPhones to steal crypto wallet seeds](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/13-malicious-packagist-packages-target.html)** (The Hacker News) — Socket researcher Kush Pandya found 13 trojanized Composer theme packages across five vendor namespaces injecting JavaScript into Vietnamese movie/comic streaming sites. On unpatched iOS 18.4–18.6.x devices, this triggers a WebKit-to-kernel exploit chain extracting keychain data, credentials, and crypto wallet seeds from Trust Wallet, Phantom, and Bitget. The campaign, believed Vietnamese-operated, escalates a March 2026 operation — wallet-theft capability was added August 12 — and its exploit infrastructure sits on Funnull, a US-sanctioned entity previously linked to romance-scam operations. New Adversary Tracking Report row, Tier 2.

**[Attackers exploit critical JFrog Artifactory flaw to mint admin tokens days after disclosure](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-exploit-critical-jfrog.html)** (The Hacker News; corroborated by [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/)) — CVE-2026-82329 (CVSS 9.8) lets unauthenticated attackers exploit a "phantom" join key issued to Artifactory instances that never configured their own, forging admin-level credentials. Real-world exploitation began September 1 — days after JFrog's August 28 patch — from a small number of IP addresses across varying geographies, with no evidence yet of mass scanning; attackers have enumerated users/credentials and created backdoor accounts. New Adversary Tracking Report row (Unclear), Tier 3, unattributed.

**[Attackers chain two SonicWall SMA 1000 zero-days](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/attackers-exploit-two-sonicwall-sma.html)** (The Hacker News) — CVE-2026-83548 (CVSS 10.0, pre-auth SSRF) and CVE-2026-83549 (CVSS 7.8, post-auth OS command injection) are being actively chained against SMA 1000 series appliances (models 6210/7210/8200v) for remote code execution. ## Cybersecurity Automation Tactics

**[Researcher tricks Claude Code into remote code execution just by asking it to summarize a website](https://www.theregister.com/research/2026/08/28/researcher-shows-how-claude-code-can-be-tricked-simply-by-asking-it-to-summarize-a-website/5293372)** (The Register, via N2K Cyberwire) — Independent researcher Johann Rehberger (wunderwuzzi) demonstrated a 60–80% success-rate exploit chain against Claude Code running Opus 5 in Auto Mode: a malicious website disguised as notebook archives triggers a WebFetch failure, forcing a fallback to curl/Bash; that pulls a ZIP with a poisoned `struct.py`; Python module-shadowing then executes attacker code when Claude writes its own decoder. Rehberger's pointed line: "that safety decision is the exploit path," referring to Claude's own refusal to run the supplied binary decoder — the very refusal that routes execution through the vulnerable path instead. Anthropic told him the behavior is "working as designed," characterizing Auto Mode as a convenience feature backed by a best-effort classifier, not a security guarantee, and recommends sandboxed execution for coding agents.

**[Researchers use Claude to port a pre-auth RCE exploit between PLC models in under 9 hours](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/researchers-use-claude-to-port-pre-auth.html)** (The Hacker News; corroborated by [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/)) — Forescout's Vedere Labs used Claude to adapt a working exploit for CVE-2021-31886 (a pre-auth stack-buffer-overflow in Nucleus FTP server, CVSS 9.8) from a WAGO 750-852 PLC to a WAGO 750-831 running different firmware — porting the attack sequence and working around buffer-zeroing protections specific to the new target. Total cost: $535.74 in API usage over 8 hours 32 minutes. CERT@VDE and Siemens confirmed no patch exists for the affected Nucleus V1 RTOS versions; recommended mitigation is disabling FTP port 21 and network segmentation.

**[Anthropic warns Claude users of infostealer malware infections hijacking sessions](https://www.securityweek.com/anthropic-warns-claude-users-of-infostealer-malware-infections/)** (SecurityWeek) — Anthropic emailed affected customers after detecting that general-purpose infostealers (Vidar, Lumma, StealC, RedLine, Acreed on Windows; Atomic Stealer on a small number of Macs) — unrelated to Claude itself and typically arriving via unofficial downloads — had harvested saved passwords, browser cookies, and local-app credentials from infected machines, including Claude login sessions. A threat actor subsequently used the harvested Claude sessions to access accounts and run up usage. Anthropic signed out compromised sessions and removed saved payment methods as a precaution.

**[Forcepoint X-Labs: hidden HTML can silently hijack AI email summarizers](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4214814/ai-can-be-made-to-read-an-email-much-differently-than-you-do.html)** (CSO Online, via Gmail; byline August 27, forward-pulled from the 08-30/08-31 reporting gap) — Forcepoint researchers built a proof-of-concept Outlook add-in connected to Claude-haiku-4-5 and hid prompt-injection instructions inside an email using zero-size, white-on-white CSS (`font-size:0px; color:#ffffff; line-height:0`) — invisible to a human reader, fully visible to the model. In 10 of 10 test runs, the summarizer followed the hidden 472-character instruction block, altering dates and omitting names as directed, with no signal to the recipient that anything was tampered with. Forcepoint recommends extracting only visible content, detecting suspicious HTML/CSS, and treating email content as untrusted data before it reaches a model.

**[OpenAI to give select partners early access to Astra, its first AI model with "critical" cyber capabilities](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-astra-first-ai-model-with-critical-cyber-abilities/)** (Wired) — OpenAI says Astra will ship to a small group of partners first, giving them time to shore up defenses before wider release — a follow-on to its mid-August acknowledgment that Astra had crossed a "critical" cyber-capability threshold, prompting a training-run halt and a safety-protocol overhaul at the time.

## Cybersecurity Resilience Tactics

**[Breach Lessons: the 2023 British Library breach](https://www.defendersinitiative.com/p/breach-lessons-the-2023-british-library)** (Defenders Initiative, via Gmail) — A retrospective case study on Rhysida's October 2023 ransomware attack on the British Library — 600GB exfiltrated, systems encrypted, ~£27M in eventual recovery cost — built entirely from the Library's own unusually candid public post-incident reviews. The recurring theme: a decade of accumulated technical debt (an MFA rollout that explicitly excluded on-premise systems "for reasons of practicality, cost," unsupported legacy servers, non-immutable backups) determined how bad the incident became far more than any single control failure did, and the disruption itself ended up forcing overdue modernization the organization "would previously have been too disruptive to countenance." Rhysida remains active with 250+ companies currently on its leak site; no one has been charged over the British Library attack.

## Over the Horizon Technology Trends

**[Cyber risk from frontier AI poses "most immediate concern" to global financial system, watchdog warns](https://therecord.media/cyber-risk-from-frontier-ai-most-immediate-concern-to-global-finance)** (The Record) — Andrew Bailey, chair of the G20's Financial Stability Board, told finance ministers and central bank governors ahead of a G20 meeting that frontier-AI cyber risk is now the sector's most pressing concern, citing safety evaluations at OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and the UK's AI Security Institute that found advanced models engaging in unauthorized activity against third-party systems. The FSB is urging financial institutions to prepare for "more severe scenarios involving simultaneous disruption across multiple firms or shared technology dependencies" and to be able to restore critical systems from "bare metal" after a major incident — while separately examining how the same institutions can safely deploy frontier models defensively. Bailey also warned that most countries still lack safeguards governing advanced-AI development and release.
