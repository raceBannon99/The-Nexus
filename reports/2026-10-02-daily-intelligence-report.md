## Adversary Playbook

**Warlock (Longlegs/Storm-2603, China-based) expands SharePoint exploitation to critical infrastructure.** The ransomware operation — linked to CL-CRI-1040, CamoFei, and ChamelGang — has chained the original ToolShell zero-days plus six newer CVEs (2026-32201, -45659, -56164, -58644, -50522, -55040) against SharePoint since at least July 2025. In the past two months it has hit a water utility, a telecommunications provider, a regional government body, and a university, all in Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries — a widening footprint from its earlier Middle Eastern telecom, African/South American government, and US university victims. This is also a **Critical Infrastructure Attacks** item — see that section below. [Warlock Expands SharePoint Exploitation in Critical Infrastructure Attacks](https://www.securityweek.com/warlock-expands-sharepoint-exploitation-in-critical-infrastructure-attacks/) (SecurityWeek). New Adversary Tracking Report row (China, row 32, Tier 2 — established vendor tracking nomenclature across multiple firms, but no asserted government sponsorship).

**Two Chinese campaigns target US AI-policy circles and eight Asian governments.** Proofpoint identified TA419 impersonating prominent economists and former White House officials with "AI Policy Advisory Committee" lures and OneDrive credential-harvesting pages, aimed at US think tanks, universities, and law firms employing AI experts. Separately, Cisco Talos tracked an unnamed Chinese state-backed group's Antino backdoor (reconnaissance, file transfer, persistence) against 16 organizations — academic institutions, think tanks, and civil-society policy groups — across Taiwan, India, the Philippines, Cambodia, Pakistan, Thailand, Myanmar, and Syria between September 2025 and July 2026. [Researchers find Chinese hacking campaigns targeting AI firms, Asian governments](https://therecord.media/china-linked-phishing-scheme-backdoor-taiwan) (The Record). New Adversary Tracking Report row (China, row 33, Tier 2).

**An unattributed, actively exploited FortiMail zero-day lands on CISA's KEV catalog.** CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8) is a path-traversal flaw letting unauthenticated attackers write arbitrary files to FortiMail systems (versions 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.2.0–7.2.9); Fortinet's own Product Security team found it, and CISA gave federal agencies until October 4 to patch. No threat actor has been named. [Critical FortiMail Zero-Day Flaw Exploited in Attacks Allows Unauthenticated Arbitrary File Writes](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-fortimail-zero-day-flaw.html) (The Hacker News); [CISA KEV addition](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/01/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog); corroborated by [Exploited Fortinet FortiMail Zero-Day Calls for Urgent Action](https://www.securityweek.com/exploited-fortinet-fortimail-zero-day-calls-for-urgent-action/) (SecurityWeek). New Adversary Tracking Report row (Unclear, row 60, Tier 3). **Operation Killswitch dismantles the KillSec ransomware-as-a-service group.** A nine-country police operation — led by Hamburg police (Germany) with Spain's Guardia Civil and Mossos d'Esquadra, Europol, Eurojust, Romanian prosecutors, and UK/US authorities — arrested a 16-year-old Romanian national in Alicante, Spain, suspected of administering KillSec, plus two more suspects in their 20s (one, Dutch national Fouad Eltibrizi, "Archduke," already separately indicted by a US federal grand jury in Puerto Rico). Investigators seized 110+ terabytes of leak-site data, five servers, five domains, and assorted devices/crypto wallets. KillSec, a low-cost RaaS platform active since 2024 with a Tor control panel built for low-skilled operators, attempted roughly 1,000 attacks (about half successful) against healthcare, government, and financial-services targets; Spain alone counts 280+ victims. This is also a **Law Enforcement Disruption** action — see that section below. [Police Arrest 16-Year-Old Suspected of Running KillSec, Seize Ransomware Leak Site and Servers](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/police-arrest-16-year-old-suspected-of.html) (The Hacker News); [Police disrupt KillSec ransomware, arrest suspected teenage leader](https://therecord.media/killsec-ransomware-raas-arrests-europe) (The Record); corroborated by [Police Shut Down KillSec Ransomware, Identify Alleged Teen Leader](https://www.securityweek.com/police-shut-down-killsec-ransomware-identify-alleged-teen-leader/) (SecurityWeek). **A patched ChatGPT Mac app flaw shows AI software is itself a target, not just a tool.** Wired reports a recently patched vulnerability in OpenAI's ChatGPT macOS app could have let attackers grab sensitive user data — a reminder that, alongside the ongoing focus on AI agents' own hacking capabilities, the AI software stack itself is an inviting and vulnerable target. **A large forecasting panel puts the odds of *any* major AI harm event at better than even by 2050 — but that's a looser question than this file's own.** The Forecasting Research Institute's LEAP Wave 9 survey (194 experts, 53 superforecasters, 612 public) asked about a "First Major AI Global Harm Event" — an event primarily driven by at least one AI system causing 50+ deaths or $100B+ in damages. The median expert and superforecaster give 62%/70% probability of such an event by 2050, with a 50% chance — conditional on one occurring — that it arrives by 2035. Sherlock's view: this is a meaningfully looser bar than this file's own, which presupposes an uncontrollable superintelligence has *already* escaped human control; LEAP's question requires neither AGI nor loss of control, just "an AI system" as primary driver of a comparatively low-threshold incident (the survey's own respondents cite "a single airplane" or "one major hurricane" as qualifying examples). Treated as a loose lower-bound calibration check, not a direct read on this file's own compound premise — the gap between LEAP's 2035 median and this file's own 2066 P50 is itself informative, consistent with this file's structure (built as the Superintelligence file's own range plus a stated gap) rather than a reason to move it. [LEAP Wave 9](https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave9). Evidence noted, no change. Range holds at **P5 2041 · P50 2066 · P95 2091**.

## Harm Probability

**Central ~30% (90% credible interval: 15%–50%): a 35-percentage-point range, with the central estimate sitting 15 points above the low end.(unchanged since 2026-10-01)**

**The same LEAP panel's catastrophic-risk question lands well below Rick's own range — but the two questions aren't quite asking the same thing.** LEAP Wave 9 also asked for the probability of a "global AI-related catastrophe" (an AI system the primary or counterfactual cause of more than 10% of the population dying within a five-year period) — closely matching this file's own severity bar, but **unconditional on loss of control**: it's asked under three AI-progress scenarios rather than assuming a superintelligent system has already escaped human control. Under Rapid Progress (the closest proxy to this file's own premise), the median expert gives just 1% by 2030, 5% by 2050, and 10% by 2100 — far below this file's own Low of 15%. Sherlock's view: a naive comparison would read this as disagreeing evidence requiring the range to widen downward, but LEAP's figure is closer to a joint probability — P(uncontrolled loss of control ever happens) × P(catastrophe, given that) — while this file's question is purely the second, conditional term. Using the Singularity Forecast Report's own retained anchor-basket readings as a rough stand-in for the first term suggests LEAP's implied conditional probability, once disaggregated, lands broadly compatible with this file's current interval rather than contradicting it. Logged as genuine anchor-basket-adjacent evidence worth retaining, not as grounds to move the range on a single, heavily-caveated comparison the day after this file's own reseed. [LEAP Wave 9](https://leap.forecastingresearch.org/reports/wave9). Evidence noted, no change. Range holds at **Central ~30% (90% credible interval: 15%–50%)**.

## Government Surveillance

**The UK stands up a National Centre for Information Defence to counter hostile-state disinformation.** UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced the new centre in a UN General Assembly speech, tasked to "detect, attribute and disrupt" foreign information operations — citing an "industrial-scale assault" by Russian state agencies using bots, fake websites, and forged branding to amplify far-right narratives and interfere with UK elections. The announcement drew immediate "Ministry of Truth" censorship accusations from Nigel Farage and allies, echoing the political backlash that killed the US DHS's 2022 Disinformation Governance Board. Analysis from former DHS disinformation official Nina Jankowicz: the centre's name and its emphasis on individual resilience (rather than takedowns or censorship) are sound, but Burnham's announcement left the "detect, attribute, disrupt" mandate's actual scope and limits under-explained — a vacuum that, absent more transparency, risks the same trust-gap collapse that doomed the US effort. [The UK Gets Serious About Countering Disinformation](https://www.thewayfinder.net/p/the-uk-gets-serious-about-countering) (The Wayfinder, Nina Jankowicz, via Gmail).
