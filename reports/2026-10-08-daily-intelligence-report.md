**[South Korea's AI-agent-suspected bank hacks widen — "Artex AI" suspected, 68,000+ exposed across 7 institutions](https://therecord.media/south-korean-bank-hacks-ai-agents)** — President Lee Jae Myung said Tuesday that "signs have emerged" AI agents were used in at least some of the recent breaches of Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank, Hana Bank, and Woori Bank, with officials suspecting the Chinese-made tool "Artex AI." Investigators have traced 33 IP addresses linked to at least 12 countries (including Japan, the US, Thailand, Vietnam, and Hong Kong), and the exposed-person count has widened to at least 68,000 across the four-plus institutions (Shinhan alone: ~25,000 customers' borrowing history, income, phone numbers, and names). This row (tracked in the Adversary Tracking Report's Unclear table since 10-06) remains Tier 3 — "Artex AI" is a suspected tool name, not a confirmed group or state sponsor. *Also covered below under AI Singularity Timeline, where its forecast implications are discussed.*

**[US posts $10 million reward for accused Chinese 'Hafnium' hacker](https://therecord.media/accused-hafnium-hacker-zhang-yu-10million-reward)** — The State Department is offering $10 million for information on Zhang Yu, a Chinese national accused — alongside already-extradited co-defendant Xu Zewei — of directing the HAFNIUM campaign (Feb. 2020–Jun. 2021) that compromised thousands of computers worldwide, including COVID-19 research stolen from US universities, immunologists, and virologists. Per the FBI, HAFNIUM targeted over 60,000 US entities and successfully compromised more than 12,700, acting at the direction of China's Ministry of State Security and the Shanghai State Security Bureau. ## Over the Horizon Technology Trends

**[Quantum computers could break today's encryption. Washington needs to prepare now.](https://cyberscoop.com/quantum-computers-could-break-todays-encryption-washington-needs-to-prepare-now/)** — In a CyberScoop op-ed, Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) argues quantum computing poses an imminent national-security threat requiring urgent congressional action, warning that "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks — adversaries stealing encrypted data today for future decryption — are an immediate vulnerability. He cites NSA's CNSA 2.0 quantum-resistant standards and the White House's federal transition deadlines (2030 for key establishment, 2031 for digital signatures), Google's internal 2029 target, and China's roughly $17.5 billion in state-backed quantum venture funding under its 15th Five-Year Plan, and calls for a comprehensive intelligence-community assessment of quantum-decryption exposure plus Defense Department network/satellite/communications reviews.

**[The Pentagon hopes to speed up 'kill chain' AI buys with 5-minute videos](https://www.wired.com/story/the-pentagon-hopes-to-speed-up-kill-chain-ai-buys-with-5-minute-videos/)** — Wired reports on the Defense Department's Tradewinds initiative, which grants "post-competitive" procurement status to AI vendors — including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — based on five-minute product-pitch videos, letting the Pentagon award contracts (some using Other Transaction agreements exempt from standard federal acquisition rules, and from congressional notification below $500 million) in as little as a week. This year's solicited focus areas shifted from the Biden administration's "responsible AI" guardrail pitches to AI that can "enhance force lethality" and "unleash" AI-enabled agents for joint targeting, including kill-chain execution — a change a DOD official says aligns with a January 2026 AI strategy memo. Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) told Wired the broader Other Transaction authority mechanism has drawn "questionable costs, mishandling of restricted information, a lack of oversight."

## Government Surveillance

**[Oklahoma judge's Flock ruling shows the power of Supreme Court's digital-evidence decision](https://therecord.media/oklahoma-flock-ruing-supreme-court-chatrie-decision)** — An Oklahoma federal judge ruled October 1 that a sheriff's deputy's warrantless search of Flock automated license-plate-reader data — used to pull 50 instances of a suspect's vehicle location across a month-long, multi-state period — was an unconstitutional "indiscriminate mass surveillance" search, directly applying the Supreme Court's June *Chatrie* geofencing decision. It's among the first rulings nationally to hold that ALPR database searches can require a warrant when used to track movement over an extended period, rather than treating plate-reader hits as plain-view camera snapshots; Flock disputes the ruling and expects it overturned on appeal. Legal scholars expect it to be the first of many *Chatrie*-driven challenges to ALPR surveillance nationwide.

## Critical Infrastructure Attacks

**[Cyber experts call on CISA to create mandatory federal OT rules](https://therecord.media/cyber-experts-call-on-cisa-require-ot-security)** — The Operational Technology Cybersecurity Coalition released a white paper urging CISA to issue a binding operational directive setting baseline cybersecurity standards for the operational technology federal agencies rely on — citing this year's water-system intrusions across 12+ states (previously tracked in this report's Iran table) as a clear warning sign, and a GAO finding that only 7 of 22 reviewed civilian agencies had met a 2024 White House deadline to inventory their OT/IoT devices. The proposed directive would require agencies to designate an accountable official for OT security, build asset inventories, segment networks, and establish incident-preparedness and backup/recovery plans. CISA declined to comment on the paper but engaged with the coalition during its drafting.

## Nation-State Cyber Policy & Law

**[Russian cyberattacks against UK are 'Putin Tax' costing $3.3 billion, says lawmaker](https://therecord.media/russia-cyberattacks-on-britain-putin-tax-graeme-downie)** — A report by Labour MP Graeme Downie, titled "The Putin Tax," estimates Russian cyberattacks, sabotage, and infrastructure threats cost the UK between £2–2.5 billion ($2.6–3.3 billion) a year — a figure the report's own authors call a floor, since the British government keeps no official estimate at all. Downie found via parliamentary questions that no government department has measured the economic impact of incidents linked to the Kremlin, including the NHS-supplier Advanced and Royal Mail ransomware attacks. The report recommends an annual government economic assessment of hostile-state cyber activity and a public-awareness campaign on the threat, arguing that without public cost figures, "it cannot weigh that burden against the cost of defending against it."

## Cybersecurity Intrusion Kill Chain Prevention Tactics

**["Both AI Skeptics and AI Enthusiasts in Cyber Are Wrong"](https://ventureinsecurity.net/p/both-ai-skeptics-and-ai-enthusiasts)** — Venture in Security's Ross Haleliuk argues that once an attacker — nation-state, cybercriminal, or AI agent — is "armed with the same tools," the right defensive question stops being *who or what* is attacking and becomes *how they break in*: the Cyber Kill Chain (Lockheed Martin, 2011) and MITRE ATT&CK frameworks, both over a decade old, remain exactly as relevant after AI as before it. His framing: AI changes exploitation *speed* and the *duty of care* defenders owe, not the fundamental defensive playbook — "twenty years ago we had two problems, patches and passwords. Today, we still have the same two problems." *Bears on [[First Principles Newsletter Tracker]] Thread 4 (Kill Chain Prevention's renewed operational feasibility) — logged there as Supports, an independent voice reaching Rick's own standing thesis without citing his essays.*

## Cybersecurity Workforce Development Tactics

**[$11 million plan for psychological support at Cyber Command gets fresh boost from lawmakers](https://therecord.media/cyber-command-mental-health-support-program-bipartisan-letters)** — Two bipartisan groups of lawmakers urged congressional appropriators to fund an $11 million "High Performance Team Training" request at US Cyber Command, following a wave of suicide deaths this summer among personnel at Cyber Command and the co-located NSA at Fort Meade. The request — the No. 2 item on the command's own Unfunded Priority List — would fund "cognitive, psychological, physiological, and spiritual sustainment for high-tempo cyber operations," with lawmakers warning that "without funding, burnout and force degradation will continue." The House approved the funding in its FY2027 defense bill; the Senate has not yet acted.

## Compliance Trends

**[Senate passes healthcare cybersecurity bill after 190 million impacted by Change Healthcare breach](https://therecord.media/senate-passes-healthcare-cyber-bill-after-change-breach)** — The Senate passed the Health Care Cybersecurity and Resiliency Act of 2026 by unanimous consent, which would require HHS to mandate minimum cybersecurity standards (including multifactor authentication) for private healthcare entities, create a joint HHS-CISA incident-response plan, and require healthcare breach notifications to disclose total victim counts. The bill followed the Change Healthcare ransomware attack that exposed 190 million Americans' health data; the American Hospital Association backed it but wants clarity on whether third-party vendors — the source of most reported healthcare breaches per the AHA — will be held to the same standard as covered entities.

## Cybersecurity Framework Trends

**[Major rules for federal contractors handling sensitive data are nearing the finish line](https://cyberscoop.com/federal-contractors-cui-cybersecurity-rules/)** — Pending federal regulations would require contractors handling "controlled unclassified information" to comply with NIST SP 800-171 cybersecurity standards (flowing down to subcontractors) and report CUI breaches within 72 hours — aligning with forthcoming CISA rules under the Cybersecurity Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act. Final rules could arrive by year-end 2026; the Aerospace Industries Association has pushed back on the 72-hour window, requesting 30 days instead.
