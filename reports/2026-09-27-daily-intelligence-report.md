**[US Military Had Close Call After Using AI for False Intelligence Report](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/18/politics/us-military-ai-false-intelligence-china-ship)** *(forward-pulled from a March 2026 incident, first reported by CNN September 18 — never previously captured in this report)*

A special-operations-command analyst's AI chatbot fused open-source intelligence with classified signals intelligence and produced an "entirely false" report claiming a Chinese ship in the Middle East was transporting nuclear-weapons-program components. The US military swung into motion — armed personnel preparing to board the vessel, aircraft in the air — before officials, digging into the report's provenance just ahead of the operation, traced the claim to the chatbot's fabrication and stood the operation down. A boarding on that mistaken basis risked spiraling into direct armed conflict between the US and China. No hostile actor was involved; the danger came entirely from human reliance on an unreliable AI system embedded in a live, high-stakes decision loop.

Seldon's read: this is a genuinely new evidence category for the Singularity Forecast Report — not a sandbox escape or an unauthorized-access incident like everything else logged there, but an AI hallucination inside institutional decision-making nearly producing a catastrophic real-world consequence, with the self-correction (caught by human review before action) serving as a modest countervailing signal. It didn't move the range on its own; logged for its category novelty. Also cross-referenced to [[First Principles Newsletter Tracker]] → Thread 5 — a clean, real-world instance of the thread's own thesis that the actual AI danger is non-sentient task-pursuit and institutional-reliance failure, not a hostile, self-aware system. Bearing: Supports.
