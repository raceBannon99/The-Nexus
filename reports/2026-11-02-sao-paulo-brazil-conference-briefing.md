# São Paulo & Brazil: What to Know Before a Week of Conference Small Talk

**Date:** 2026-11-02
**Question:** I'm in São Paulo, Brazil this week speaking at a cyber conference. Provide a summary of the key things I should know about the city and the country so that I can appear to have an interest at events this week.

---

## The Answer (Agent Bradlee)

Brazil has just come out of a close and bitter election, and that will shape almost every conversation this week. On October 25, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term, beating Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, by 51.79% to 48.21%. That is about 4.4 million votes out of more than 124 million valid votes cast. Half the room voted for one man and half for the other. A visitor who says they followed the race and doesn't pick a side will come across as informed. One who offers an opinion will lose half the room.

Second, Brazilian football had a bad year. The national team went out of the 2026 World Cup in the round of 16, losing 2–1 to Norway, with both goals from Erling Haaland. Commentators called it the team's worst World Cup since 1990. The federation kept coach Carlo Ancelotti anyway, betting on him through 2030. Sympathize and don't joke about it. Locally, the better question is which São Paulo club someone supports: Corinthians, Palmeiras, São Paulo FC or Santos.

Third, the city will be busy. Today, November 2, is Finados (All Souls' Day), a national holiday when families visit cemeteries. The Formula 1 São Paulo Grand Prix runs November 6–8 at Interlagos, with the race Sunday at 2 p.m. local time. Traffic, hotels and dinner reservations will all be tighter than usual. Two more national holidays follow this month: Republic Day on November 15 and Black Consciousness Day on November 20.

For a cyber audience, the strongest local topic is Pix, the central bank's instant-payment system. Most Brazilian adults use it, Brazilians are proud of it, and it is now the country's main fraud target. Three major incidents in nine months followed the same pattern: attackers went after a third-party provider connected to Pix rather than after Pix itself. In July 2025, more than R$800 million was diverted through C&M Software. In January 2026, Banco do Nordeste suspended Pix after an attack on a provider. In March 2026, BTG Pactual suspended Pix after a theft one Brazilian outlet put at R$100 million, most of it recovered. Researchers also flagged a new Android trojan, PixRevolution, that takes over Pix transfers in real time. A homegrown fraud crew that Google, CrowdStrike and Trend Micro each track under a different name (the campaign is called Breeze Comet) poses as IT support to push fraudulent Pix and Boleto payments. Brazil is also one of the most-targeted countries for ransomware, ranked second worldwide for LockBit 5.0 victims in early 2026 according to one vendor. Mention the privacy law (LGPD) and its regulator (ANPD) by name; it signals homework.

The city itself is easy to talk about. Metro São Paulo has more than 22 million people and is Latin America's financial capital, with Faria Lima and Paulista as its main business avenues. It has the largest Japanese community outside Japan, centered in Liberdade, plus deep Italian and Lebanese roots. Paulistanos will tell you their pizza beats New York's. They enjoy their rivalry with Rio, which casts São Paulo as the city that works.

Avoid three mistakes. Don't speak Spanish; the language is Portuguese. Don't use the "OK" hand sign, which is obscene in Brazil; the thumbs-up is everywhere. Don't raise US trade tensions, which have been raw since 2025. In July 2026 Washington imposed new 25% tariffs on many Brazilian goods, and talks between Lula and Trump were still unresolved going into the election. Let your hosts raise it.

A few words of Portuguese go a long way: *obrigado* (thank you), *tudo bem?* (how's it going?), *prazer* (nice to meet you) and *saúde!* (cheers). Expect warm greetings, long handshakes, small talk before business, and meetings that run late, partly because the traffic is legendary. Keep your phone out of sight on the street and use Uber or 99 rather than hailing cabs.

- **First round (Oct. 4):** Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro advanced to the runoff.

- **Background (general coverage, pre-cutoff):** Jair Bolsonaro was convicted in September 2025 of plotting a coup after the 2022 election. That conviction is why his son, not he, was the right's candidate.

### US–Brazil relations
- Lula and Trump met at the White House on May 7, 2026, and agreed to set up a bilateral tariff working group.
- On July 15, 2026, USTR imposed 25% tariffs on many Brazilian products. The Lula government called this a severe setback.
- Lula and Trump spoke by phone on Aug. 21, 2026, and Lula pushed for renewed talks. No resolution was found in coverage through October.
- Open issues include digital trade (Brazil blocked renewal of the WTO e-commerce moratorium) and Brazil's ethanol tariffs.

### Football
- 2026 World Cup: group-stage draw with Morocco (1–1), wins over Scotland (3–0) and Haiti (3–0), a 2–1 win over Japan in the round of 32, then a 2–1 loss to Norway in the round of 16 (Haaland scored both).
- Brazil finished fifth in South American qualifying, a historic low.
- Ancelotti was retained through 2030. Former striker Ronaldo publicly criticized his tactics.

### This week in the city
- **Nov. 2:** Finados (All Souls' Day), a national holiday.
- **Nov. 5:** CISOXBrazil 2026, São Paulo (aggregator listing).
- **Nov. 6–8:** F1 São Paulo Grand Prix at Interlagos. Race Sunday, Nov. 8, at 14:00 local time.

### Cyber landscape
- **Pix incidents:**
  - **July 2025:** C&M Software. More than R$800M diverted and three institutions suspended. Afterwards the Central Bank extended its anomaly-detection requirements to all Pix participants.
  - **January 2026:** Banco do Nordeste suspended Pix after an attack on a third-party provider.
  - **March 22, 2026:** BTG Pactual suspended Pix. Reported losses differ: US$18M in one source, R$100M in another, with most of it recovered.
- **Pix malware:**
  - **PixRevolution:** an Android trojan that hijacks Pix transfers in real time (Zimperium, March 2026).
  - **Banana RAT:** hijacks Pix QR codes using NF-e (electronic invoice) lures.
- **Ransomware against government:**
  - Gang claims against SECONT (May 2026) and intranet.gov.br (Aug. 2026, attributed to The Gentlemen).
  - A claim against Receita Federal by Emperador (Sept. 23, 2026).
  - All three are leak-site claims, not confirmed breaches.
- **Scale:** 646 ransomware incidents in June 2026, up 33% year on year (Brazilian trade press). Brazil was the #2 target country for LockBit 5.0 in Q1 2026 (SOCRadar).
- **Policy (general coverage, partly pre-cutoff):** the LGPD privacy law is enforced by the ANPD. The national strategy is E-Ciber, coordinated by the National Cybersecurity Committee (CNCiber). This search found no 2026 update on a standalone national cyber agency.

### City and country basics (general coverage, uncited)
- **São Paulo:** about 12M people in the city and 22M+ in the metro area. Home of the B3 exchange. Largest Japanese diaspora in the world (Liberdade), plus large Italian and Lebanese communities. Landmarks: MASP, Ibirapuera Park, Avenida Paulista, the Mercadão market (known for its mortadella sandwich), and the Vila Madalena and Pinheiros neighborhoods.
- **Names:** *paulistano* means someone from the city, *paulista* someone from the state.
- **Brazil:** about 213M people, the largest economy in Latin America. Brasília is the capital. The language is Portuguese.

## First Principles (Agent Euclid)

What does a visitor need to seem genuinely interested? Interest shows when you know *what people here are already talking about this week*. General trivia doesn't do it. So the briefing should rank items by how likely they are to come up and how costly a mistake would be. Three things are close to certain to come up:
1. The election, decided eight days ago by about 3.6 points.
2. The F1 weekend, which disrupts the city.
3. Pix, at a cyber conference specifically.

Football is a near-certain icebreaker. Errors are expensive on two points: language (speaking Spanish suggests you think Brazil is "Latin America, generically") and politics (any opinion alienates about half the room). The best-value move for a speaker is to tie the talk to a Brazilian example. The Pix provider pattern is ideal: it is local, recent and technically substantive, and it is about supply-chain trust, which travels to any audience.

Use the C&M R$800M+ figure only if needed, since it is the most widely reported.
