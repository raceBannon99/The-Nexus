## Popper — How could we be wrong?

**Re-reviewed for the update pass**, since resolving "Marco Morano" and adding book/Canon-status data both touch conclusions from the original version. Four points worth taking seriously:

1. **This is a snapshot, not a guarantee.** Conference schedules — especially the "additional programs" tier below Briefings/Trainings — are known to shift in the final week before an event: a flight gets rebooked, an author drops out, a slot moves by 30 minutes. Nothing on Black Hat's page carries a "last updated" timestamp, so there's no way to know how fresh this pull is relative to the live system. Treat this table as accurate as of today, July 27, and worth a quick re-check against the printed on-site program or the Black Hat mobile app once you're at Mandalay Bay. Unchanged from the original review.
2. **The "Marco Morano" flag is resolved, not just explained away.** Bibliography research turned up a real, well-credentialed author named Marco Morana whose most recent book (*Blockchain Application Security*) fits the profile exactly, and a second, independent name discrepancy in the same schedule ("Tony Martin" for the hyphenated "Tony Martin-Vegue") suggests a genuine pattern — Black Hat's schedule system appears to compress or misspell some names — rather than two unrelated coincidences. This is inference, not confirmation from Black Hat itself, so it's presented as "almost certainly" rather than fact, but it's a substantially stronger position than the original "no results" flag.
3. **The CyberCanon status column has a real reliability gap, and it should be named plainly rather than smoothed over.** Direct site-search verification succeeded for 11 of the 14 signings (Howard, Armstrong-Smith, Acohido, Shostack, Gourley, Davidoff/Durrin, Rajan, Santos/Lazzara/Thurner, Morana, Teri Green, plus the general absence check) but the tool became unreliable partway through this session — repeated attempts to search "Andy Ellis" and "Tony Martin(-Vegue)" on cybercanon.org's own search widget failed to register input across ~15 attempts, for reasons that were eventually traced to the page's search box shifting position between loads (not a data problem, a browser-automation timing issue). Rather than guess at those two authors' Canon status, both are marked "not confirmed either way" in the table. Helen Patton's Hall of Fame status rests on a secondary source's explicit claim rather than a direct cybercanon.org re-verification this pass — flagged in the table rather than presented as equivalent-confidence to the site-confirmed entries.
4. **Pete Green's book is a real gap, not an oversight.** Sherlock could not find any cybersecurity-authored book under this name — there is a security professional named Pete Green on LinkedIn, but nothing connecting him to a published book. This is worth a direct on-site check if the slot matters to you; it may be a very recent or self-published title that doesn't surface in general web search.

This is reasoned judgment based on how conference-adjacent programming typically behaves in the final week, not a measured track record of Black Hat's bookstore specifically — there's no dataset tracking year-over-year signing-schedule change rates to anchor it more tightly.
