## First principles (Euclid)

Two things have to be fundamentally true for any outreach here to work, and they point at the same two people:

1. **Someone has to have the standing to say yes to an outside speaker's request** — a classroom visit, an informal meet-and-greet, an introduction to the rest of the department. In a five-person department, that's structurally the chair: Hernandez.
2. **Someone has to actually own the subject matter** — the person whose curriculum, students, and daily teaching load this speaks to most directly. That's structurally the person the college itself designates as the Cybersecurity Option's advisor: Zilovic.

Everything else — Dougherty, Marzelli, Taggart, the 22-person advisory board — is a widening circle around those two, useful for a broader visit but not the critical path. The department's small size is itself informative: this isn't a specialized cybersecurity faculty separate from general CIS faculty, it's a five-person CIS department where cybersecurity is one of several tracks. Treat all five as "people who teach cybersecurity courses at some point" rather than assuming a dedicated cybersecurity faculty exists to be found.

## Where this could be wrong (Popper)

- **The full-time roster isn't a semester-specific instructor list.** Course catalogs and program pages list who's authorized/qualified to teach in a program, not who's actually assigned to CISM215 this fall. Community college programs — especially in specialized, in-demand tracks like ethical hacking and computer forensics — often lean on adjunct instructors for exactly those specialty courses, and adjuncts are far less likely to appear in a public full-time-faculty catalog page. **I could not confirm section-level instructor assignments**: Atlantic Cape's public course-search tool (`acccdtsfss.atlantic.edu`) blocked both WebFetch (connection refused) and the Chrome extension (site permission denied), and general web search returned nothing semester-specific. If Rick wants certainty on who's teaching Ethical Hacking or Computer Forensics *this term specifically*, that's a direct-ask question for Hernandez or Zilovic, not something resolvable from outside.
- **Hernandez's title is inconsistent across sources** — "Department Chair, Professor" on the college's own current directory page, but "Vice President, Academic Affairs" on an org-chart aggregator (The Org) and a slightly older ZoomInfo listing. The directory page is the more current, authoritative source and is treated as correct above, but it's plausible he was promoted and the directory hasn't caught up, or that the org-chart site is simply stale — either way, worth a quick confirmation rather than assuming he's still the hands-on department contact.
- **The two unconfirmed LinkedIn profiles (Dougherty, Taggart) are a real risk if guessed at.** Rather than pick the "closest-sounding" match from a page of unrelated people with the same name, both are left blank. Guessing wrong and reaching out to a stranger is worse than no LinkedIn link at all.
- **The advisory board's roles are now mostly identified, but confidence varies and none are re-confirmed as currently active.** The casino-security trio (Fox, Simonsen, Seaman) and the academic partners (Kokalj-Filipović especially) are corroborated across multiple independent sources; a few (Zambotti, Pacanowski, Schillo) rest on a single plausible-but-not-certain match against a common name; two (Kneisel, Newsome) remain genuinely unidentified. The catalog page itself doesn't date-stamp membership, so "on the list" isn't the same as "still serving" — treat the high-confidence names as strong leads, not confirmed appointments.

## What's likely to happen next (Seldon)

Not much genuine forecasting applies to a contact list, but the one real uncertainty — will outreach to Zilovic and Hernandez actually land a meeting before the conference — is worth sizing: both addresses are active, staffed faculty emails with posted office hours (not general department inboxes), and outside speakers reaching out to a program ahead of a related regional conference is a familiar, low-friction ask for academic staff. Given that, the range for a response before the event runs from roughly even odds to quite likely — call it a 55–85% chance of at least one reply, median around 70% — with the spread driven mostly by lead time (a request landing a week out is far more likely to get a scheduling response than one landing the day before) rather than by anything about the ask itself. Emailing both together this week, rather than close to the conference date, is the single biggest lever on that range.
