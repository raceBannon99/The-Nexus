# Sources That Argue AI Catastrophe Is Further Off, or Less Likely

<img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raceBannon99/The-Nexus/main/assets/first-principles-consulting-logo.png" align="right" width="220">

**Question:** For the AI timeline, we have many sources that highlight evidence that P(doom) is coming sooner rather than later. Recommend other sources that take the opposite view.

## The Answer

Add three skeptical feeds to the daily report now: Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor's *AI as Normal Technology*, Epoch AI's newsletter, and David Thorstad's *Reflective Altruism*. Trial two more: Timothy B. Lee's *Understanding AI* and Gary Marcus's *Marcus on AI*. Seven works belong on a one-time reading list, led by the Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament, in which superforecasters put AI-caused extinction by 2100 at 0.38% against AI experts' 3%.

The problem is real and measurable. Across the four date-based forecast files, roughly 95 evidence entries are tagged as pulling timelines sooner and about 10 later; the Singularity file alone runs 75 to 7. None of the 24 active sources is built to carry the skeptical case. The AI Alignment Forum, the main source for these forecasts, is a safety-research community where short timelines are a common working assumption. Searches of every published Nexus report find Narayanan cited once and Andrew Ng twice, and never Gary Marcus, Robin Hanson, David Thorstad, Nora Belrose, Yann LeCun, Melanie Mitchell, or Rodney Brooks.

The skeptics are not a single camp, and the strongest ones are worth reading because they engage this month's evidence directly. Epoch AI tested whether frontier models can independently rediscover a recent machine-learning advance; their best efforts captured 15% of the human result, and they falsely claimed success. That speaks directly to the METR acceleration finding that moved three Nexus forecasts this month. Narayanan and Kapoor argue that p(doom) numbers "launder vague intuitions and fears through a facade of quantification." Their 13,000-word essay reads this year's agent-escape incidents as security failures more than alignment crises. Thorstad's series concludes that the leading arguments that a dangerous AI couldn't be shut down "do not succeed," which, if right, lowers estimates of how likely catastrophe is. Lee argues that a sim-to-real gap will keep AI from transforming most professions by 2027 or 2028.

Two cautions. First, the goal is calibration, not balance for its own sake. A skeptical post should move a forecast only when its evidence is as strong as the alarming evidence it answers. Adding skeptical feeds widens the evidence the forecasts see; it does not decide anything. Second, several of the most prominent skeptics have their own track-record problems. Marcus has called the limits of large language models early and often, and capabilities kept arriving, so he is a trial with a strict filter rather than a full source.

This also bears on your own published position. "The Doomsday Talk Isn't Over" defends a quantified estimate (central ~30%, a 15–50% range) against a colleague who said doomsayers have a financial incentive to hype. Narayanan and Kapoor's challenge is different and harder: not motive, but whether any method can calibrate that number at all. The 0.38% to 3% gap between careful forecaster groups, both below your 15% floor, is the clearest sign the range rests on judgment more than data. Your thesis about how AI causes harm is untouched; the confidence in the headline number is what this complicates.

If the five feeds are adopted, expect skeptical evidence in roughly half of daily runs rather than almost none. Treat each item the way the forecast files already treat a disagreeing source: weigh it, and let it widen a range, never ignore it.

## What Must Be Fundamentally True? (Euclid)

A forecast is only as good as the evidence it sees, and an intake that systematically over-samples one direction will drift in that direction even if every individual judgment is careful. Three things follow.

1. **Sources should be chosen for the questions they answer, not their conclusions.** The five forecasts ask how fast capabilities grow, whether that growth compounds, how fast it reaches the world, whether control can be maintained, and how likely catastrophe is. The current intake is strong on the first two (lab disclosures, METR, Alignment Forum) and weak on the last three. The best skeptical sources are strongest exactly there: diffusion and bottlenecks (Narayanan & Kapoor, Lee), controllability (Thorstad, Belrose & Pope), and calibration of the probabilities themselves (the tournament).
2. **Measurement beats commentary in both directions.** Epoch's InnovationEval and the tournament are measurements; most skeptical writing, like most alarmed writing, is argument. A measured null result should weigh as much as a measured positive one.
3. **The Harm Probability seed is most exposed.** Its 15%–55% interval sits entirely above both tournament groups' extinction medians (0.38%, 3%). The questions aren't identical (the seed is conditional on loss of control, with a 10%-of-humanity bar), so this isn't a contradiction. But it means the range rests heavily on judgment, and the file has logged no skeptical evidence at all.

## How Could We Be Wrong? (Popper)

1. **This could be false balance.** If 2026's evidence genuinely points sooner, adding skeptics to even out the count distorts rather than corrects. The Sooner tags may simply be accurate.
2. **Skeptics have incentives and track records too.** Narayanan and Kapoor have built a brand and a new lab on the "normal technology" view. Marcus's capability predictions have repeatedly missed. Treating skeptics as neutral correctives repeats the mistake in the other direction.
3. **Epoch conflicts with an existing rule.** `Sources.md` says not to add Epoch for the AI-timeline categories because it overlaps the Alignment Forum. Recommending it now needs a reason, not an exception by default.
4. **More feeds means more noise.** Marcus posts near-daily, much of it market commentary. Every daily run already pulls 24 sources.
5. **Skeptical sources may rarely produce loggable evidence.** Arguments that a bottleneck exists don't usually come with dated findings the way lab disclosures do, so they may contribute little even if added.

## What Is Likely to Happen Next? (Seldon)

**On Objection 1 (false balance):** accepted as a constraint, not a reason to skip the change. The fix is to widen what the forecasts see, not to set quotas. Skeptical items get logged and weighed by the same rules as everything else, and they move a range only on comparable strength. If they consistently fail that bar, the Sooner tags were right, and that becomes a finding rather than an assumption.

**On Objection 2 (incentives):** stood by, partially. Every source gets weighed on its reasoning and corroboration, the standard the forecast files adopted on 2026-10-20. That is also why Marcus is a trial with a filter (only posts making a dated, testable claim about capability limits) while Epoch, which measures in both directions, is a full add.

**On Objection 3 (Epoch rule):** revised. The 10-16 rule assumed Epoch would duplicate the Alignment Forum, but Epoch publishes its own measurements (InnovationEval, cost curves, automation tests) that the Forum only sometimes relays. Recommend amending the rule: add Epoch's newsletter, crediting only items that report a new Epoch measurement.

**On Objection 4 (noise):** addressed by the trial structure and filters below, and by reviewing all five on the existing 30-run cadence.

**On Objection 5 (low yield):** expected, and priced into the forecasts below. A low hit rate is acceptable for a source added to fill a gap; that is the standard already applied to LEAP and the weekly newsletters.

**Expected contribution, per daily run** (reasoned judgment from posting cadence and topical share, not measured data):
- *AI as Normal Technology*: about 10% to 35% of runs, median around 20%: a 25-point span, with the median 10 points above the low end.
- Epoch AI newsletter (new measurements only): about 15% to 45%, median around 25%: a 30-point span, median 10 points above the low end.
- *Reflective Altruism*: about 3% to 15%, median around 6%: a 12-point span, median 3 points above the low end.
- *Understanding AI* (trial): about 5% to 20%, median around 10%: a 15-point span, median 5 points above the low end.
- *Marcus on AI* (trial, strict filter): about 5% to 30%, median around 12%: a 25-point span, median 7 points above the low end.

Combined, treating the five as independent, at least one skeptical item should appear in about a third to four-fifths of runs, with a median around half, against near zero today. **No standing forecast moves today.** This engagement recommends sources rather than weighing new evidence. The reading list should be worked through in a dedicated pass, with each item logged in the relevant Evidence Log; the tournament and Thorstad items belong first in the Harm Probability file.

**Review trigger:** at the 30-run review, compare the Sooner-to-Later tag ratio across the forecast files. If it is still above roughly 5 to 1 with these feeds active, the imbalance is more likely real-world signal than intake bias.
